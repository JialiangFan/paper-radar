# Demystifying Long Chain-of-Thought Reasoning

## Topic: Long CoT reasoning analysis

**Authors**: Edward Yeo, Yuxuan Tong, Morry Niu, Graham Neubig, Xiang Yue
**Affiliations**: IN.AI, Tsinghua University, Carnegie Mellon University
**Date**: February 5, 2025
**arXiv**: 2502.03373

---

## Background

Large Language Models (LLMs) have shown impressive capabilities in complex reasoning domains such as mathematics and programming. A key technique enabling these capabilities is chain-of-thought (CoT) prompting, which guides models to generate intermediate reasoning steps before producing a final answer.

Despite these advances, LLMs still struggle with highly complex tasks — mathematical competitions, PhD-level scientific QA, and software engineering — even when equipped with CoT. OpenAI's o1 models demonstrated a breakthrough by leveraging long CoT (long chain-of-thought), whose distinguishing feature is the ability to scale inference compute: models can recognize and correct mistakes, break down difficult steps, and iterate over alternative approaches, leading to substantially longer and more structured reasoning processes.

Reinforcement learning (RL) has emerged as a critical method for developing long CoT capabilities. However, the conditions under which long CoTs emerge and the necessary RL training design choices remain poorly understood.

---

## Limitations & Research Problem

1. **Unclear emergence conditions for long CoT**: While prior works (DeepSeek-R1, Kimi k1.5, QwQ, TinyZero) have attempted to replicate o1-like long CoT, a comprehensive mechanistic understanding is lacking.
2. **Ambiguous role of SFT vs. RL**: How supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on long CoT data shapes RL outcomes, and whether SFT is even necessary, has not been systematically studied.
3. **RL training instability**: Under naive reward designs (classic binary reward), CoT length grows unboundedly, eventually exceeding the context window and causing training accuracy to collapse.
4. **Scarcity of verifiable reward signals**: High-quality human-annotated verifiable data for reasoning tasks is limited, making RL scaling difficult.
5. **Difficulty measuring emergent behaviors**: Core long CoT behaviors such as error correction and branching/backtracking are hard to quantify and confirm as genuinely emergent during RL.

---

## Contributions

This paper systematically investigates the mechanics of long CoT reasoning and presents four main findings:

1. **SFT is not strictly necessary but simplifies training and improves efficiency**: Long CoT SFT initialization enables models to reach a higher performance ceiling and makes subsequent RL improvement substantially easier, whereas short CoT SFT does not.

2. **Reasoning capabilities tend to emerge with increased training compute, but not stably**: The authors introduce the Cosine Reward with a repetition penalty to stabilize CoT length growth and prevent reward hacking via repetition.

3. **Scaling verifiable reward signals is critical for RL**: Leveraging noisy, web-extracted solutions (e.g., WebInstruct) with appropriate filtering shows strong potential, especially for out-of-distribution (OOD) tasks such as STEM reasoning; mixing MATH and WebInstruct achieves the best average performance across benchmarks.

4. **Core abilities like error correction are latently present in base models**: RL can effectively incentivize these skills, but doing so for complex tasks demands significant compute, and measuring their emergence requires a nuanced approach.

## Methodology

### Problem Formulation

- Given a query $x$, the model produces output sequence $y$. The chain-of-thought $\text{CoT}(y) \subseteq y$ refers to the reasoning trace portion of the output.
- **Long CoT** is defined as output that is not only longer than average but also exhibits:
  - **Branching and Backtracking**: systematically exploring multiple solution paths and reverting when a path fails
  - **Error Validation and Correction**: detecting inconsistencies in intermediate steps and taking corrective actions

### Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)

- **Data construction**:
  - *Distillation*: rejection-sampling from QwQ-32B-Preview (N=32–256 candidates), keeping responses with correct final answers
  - *Construction*: using an Action Prompting framework with primitive actions (clarify, decompose, solution_step, reflection, answer) orchestrated by a short CoT model, with reflection steps generated by o1-mini-0912
- **Key finding**: Models distilled from emergent long CoT patterns generalize significantly better than those trained on constructed patterns, achieving 15–50% relative improvements on OOD benchmarks (AIME 2024, MMLU-Pro-1k). Constructed patterns also failed to benefit from RL.

### Reinforcement Learning (RL)

- **Algorithm**: PPO (Proximal Policy Optimization) is used as the default; REINFORCE++ was explored but found significantly more unstable.
- **Reward design**:
  - *Classic Reward*: binary +1/0 for correct/incorrect answers. Leads to unstable CoT length growth — models eventually exceed context windows, causing accuracy collapse (observed in both Llama-3.1-8B and Qwen2.5-Math-7B).
  - *Cosine Reward*: a piecewise cosine function taking generation length $L_\text{gen}$ as input. Correct CoTs receive higher rewards for shorter lengths (incentivizing efficiency); incorrect CoTs receive milder penalties for longer lengths (encouraging the model to keep thinking when uncertain). An exceed-length penalty $r_e$ is added when $L_\text{gen} = L_\text{max}$.
  - *N-gram Repetition Penalty*: a sparse penalty applied to repeated token n-grams across the trajectory. Applied with a low temporal discount factor for locality, enabling the model to learn which specific tokens to avoid repeating.
- **Discount factors**: Different reward components (correctness, repetition) benefit from different discount factors $\gamma$. A lower $\gamma$ for the correctness reward increases branching frequency but can cause short-term thinking; a higher $\gamma$ for correctness combined with a lower $\gamma$ for repetition was found optimal.

### Scaling Verifiable Reward Signals

- **WebInstruct-462k**: a deduplicated subset of WebInstruct (web-crawled QA pairs) derived via MinHash
- **Verifier comparison**:
  - Rule-based verifier on a filtered prompt set (short-form answers only) achieves the best performance
  - Model-based verifier on the unfiltered set performs comparably on some benchmarks but worse overall
- **SFT with noisy data**: Adding WebInstruct to SFT (50% MATH + 50% WebIT) yields a 510% absolute accuracy gain on MMLU-Pro-1k over MATH-only SFT, and achieves best average across benchmarks

### RL from the Base Model

- Follows the setup of Zeng et al. (2025) using Qwen2.5-Math-7B with PPO and a rule-based verifier on ~8k MATH level 3–5 questions
- **Findings**:
  - RL boosts accuracy on MATH-500 but does not meaningfully increase reflection keyword frequencies ("wait", "recheck", "alternatively", "retry", "however")
  - CoT length increases after an initial drop, but the KL divergence simultaneously decreases — suggesting length growth may be driven by the KL penalty reverting to base model behavior rather than genuine exploration
  - RL from long CoT SFT significantly outperforms RL from base model (+8.7% on average), with the SFT model alone already yielding strong performance

### Long CoT in Pre-training Data

- The authors hypothesize that long CoT behaviors (branching, error correction) may originate from human dialogue on internet discussion forums, where multi-user exchanges display backtracking and error correction patterns — a potential latent source of long CoT in pre-training corpora (OpenWebMath)

### Training Setup

- **Base models**: Llama-3.1-8B (general) and Qwen2.5-Math-7B (math-specialized)
- **SFT data**: 7,500 MATH training samples (with ground-truth answers for rejection sampling)
- **RL framework**: OpenRLHF; context window 16K tokens; temperature 0.7, top-p 0.95 for evaluation
- **Evaluation benchmarks**:
  - MATH-500: in-domain math (12,500 competition problems, 500-sample test subset)
  - AIME 2024: out-of-domain math (30 problems from American Invitational Mathematics Examination)
  - TheoremQA: out-of-domain STEM (800 samples, 350+ theorems across Math, EE&CS, Physics, Finance)
  - MMLU-Pro-1k: out-of-domain general reasoning (1,000-sample i.i.d. subset of MMLU-Pro)

### Key Takeaways Summary

| # | Finding |
|---|---------|

| 3.2 | Long CoT SFT initialization makes further RL improvement easier; short CoT SFT does not |

| 4.1 | CoT length does not always scale up stably under classic reward |
