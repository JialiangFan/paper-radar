# Harness VLA: Steering Frozen VLAs into Reliable Manipulation Primitives via Memory-Guided Agents

> arXiv: 2607.08448 | Year: 2026

## Topic
Memory-guided agent harnessing frozen VLAs

## Background
Language-conditioned manipulation demands both precise contact-rich control and robust reasoning over language, scenes, and long horizons. End-to-end VLA models learn strong local visuomotor skills directly from robot trajectories, but are reliable only inside the trajectory distribution they were trained on. Under deployment perturbations — semantic retargeting, goal re-binding, spatial-layout shifts, unstable local contacts — the policy tends to repeat a training-time behavior even when the instruction or scene binding has changed, and a single unstable contact failure can derail an entire monolithic rollout. LLM coding agents (Code as Policies, ProgPrompt, and recent multimodal/agentic variants) supply complementary semantic and compositional reasoning, but purely analytic primitives — deterministic kinematic or model-based controllers such as IK transport, wrist rotation, base motion, gripper opening and release — remain poorly suited to irregular grasping, constrained placement, and articulated-object manipulation. This paper resolves the two lines into an asymmetric division of labor under an agent-harness view.

## Limitations & Research Problem
- **Limitation:** Monolithic VLAs absorb language grounding, long-horizon composition, and low-level control into one policy and collapse out of distribution (on LIBERO-Pro: OpenVLA 0.0%, π₀ 0.3%, π₀.₅ 11.0%). On the agent side, scaling typically means expanding the skill library, while analytic primitives alone cannot handle contact-rich phases. Two gaps recur in prior agentic work: task-specific execution traces are rarely represented as reusable, parameterized memory that can be re-grounded under new spatial layouts, and failure knowledge is seldom distilled into a Global Memory that prevents repeating known empty grasps, false successes, or unstable staging choices.
- **Problem:** Can a memory-guided agent, by orchestrating a *fixed* primitive library, lift a frozen VLA from a local contact specialist to a reliable long-horizon, perturbation-robust manipulation system — **without fine-tuning the VLA and without expanding the primitive vocabulary at deployment time**?

## Contributions
- Harness VLA: a memory-augmented agentic framework that exposes a frozen VLA as a **single retryable contact-rich primitive** (`VLA_ACT`) and composes it with fixed analytic primitives for grounding, staging, transport, navigation, and release, extending a pretrained VLA beyond its original trajectory distribution without VLA fine-tuning or deployment-time primitive expansion.
- An empirical analysis of *why a small fixed primitive library suffices* when the planner learns how to use it: repeated planner-staged invocations can reframe brittle VLA attempts, while analytic primitives solve most of the non-contact structure around each contact-rich phase.
- Strong results across standard and perturbed tabletop manipulation, household kitchen manipulation, and clean-to-randomized transfer: +38.6 and +25.4 percentage points over the strongest relevant baselines on LIBERO-Pro and RoboCasa365, and 58.4% on RoboTwin C2R, while preserving competitive standard-LIBERO performance.

## Methodology
- **Problem formulation.** A rigid-body physics engine (MuJoCo via Robosuite) drives the environment. At each timestep the observation tuple is o_t = (agent-view RGB I_t^rgb, co-aligned metric depth I_t^d, proprioceptive state q_t). A task is a natural-language description ℓ plus a binary completion predicate 𝒢, exposed only as a sparse success signal at episode termination.
- **Agentic execution loop.** A rollout is an autoregressive, turn-based interaction between a high-level planner Π and the physics engine. Rather than a separate hierarchical tier, the frozen VLA f_θ and all deterministic operational-space controllers are unified into one predefined primitive library 𝒫. At each turn Π consumes o_t, ℓ, and retrieved context from both memories, and emits a structured JSON invocation c_t ∈ 𝒫; the engine executes until that primitive's internal post-condition is met and returns o_{t+1}, iterating until 𝒢 is satisfied or a step budget is exhausted.
- **The harness (runtime contract).** A REPL-style interface that exposes primitive schemas, serializes decisions as JSON commands, executes primitives, refreshes RGB-D and proprioceptive observations, logs traces, retrieves Task Specific and Global Memory, enforces reset and budget policies, and checks progress through the benchmark predicate.
- **Two-phase agent lifecycle.**
  - *Exploratory Bootstrapping.* On a single reference instantiation of a task, the agent explores autonomously with exclusive access to a `RESET` primitive and a generous wall-clock budget, iterating over staging orders, pre-contact poses, `VLA_ACT` invocation timings, and early-return thresholds. On success, the verified primitive sequence is serialized to JSONL — with concrete spatial coordinates replaced by symbolic perception queries so the sequence is reusable across layouts — and stored in **Task Specific Memory**. Generalized heuristics are committed to a persistent **Global Memory** aggregating *success rules* (e.g. prompting strategies that use the full task instruction) and *failure models* (e.g. detecting empty-grasp executions and false success detections).
  - *Deployment Evaluation.* On unseen environment variations (position swaps, instruction redirections, multiple initial seeds), `RESET` is **completely disabled** and the step budget is significantly shortened. The planner retrieves the precomputed JSONL trace, dynamically re-grounds it against live RGB-D, and executes deterministically under the accumulated success rules and failure models. All reported benchmark numbers come from this strict phase.
- **Unified primitive interface (Table 1).** The shared manipulation interface holds six analytic primitives plus one VLA primitive: composite `MOVE_TO` and `MOVE_POSE` (world-frame Cartesian target / co-varying pose via an embedded solver), atomic `ROTATE_WRIST`, `ROTATE_PITCH`, `SET_GRIPPER`, `RELEASE`, and `VLA_ACT`. RoboCasa365 adds two mobile-base primitives, `NAVIGATE_TO` and `MOVE_BASE`, for kitchen-scale staging. `RESET` is used only during bootstrapping and is not counted as a manipulation primitive. Crucially, the vocabulary is fixed before evaluation — the planner cannot invent new primitives at deployment time.
- **VLA-backed contact primitive.** `VLA_ACT` receives a task-conditioned prompt and an early-return predicate τ; the frozen VLA f_θ emits action chunks until τ is satisfied or the chunk budget is exhausted. Across benchmarks it covers grasping, constrained placement, fixture actuation, button pressing, drawer or door manipulation, and insertion, while semantic grounding, spatial re-binding, navigation, re-staging, and long-horizon composition stay under planner control.
- **Planner backbones.** Two instantiations, Codex and Claude Code (CC), share the same harness, memory interface, primitive library, frozen-VLA interface, and evaluation protocols, differing only in the planner backbone.

## Experiments & Key Numbers
- **Setup.** Four benchmark families: LIBERO and LIBERO-Pro (tabletop), RoboCasa365 (household kitchen with mobile staging), RoboTwin C2R (bimanual clean-to-randomized transfer). `VLA_ACT` backends are the RLinf-released `pi05_libero130_fullshot` π₀.₅-SFT checkpoint (π_RLinf) for LIBERO/LIBERO-Pro, the frozen RLDX-1 RoboCasa checkpoint for RoboCasa365, and the post-trained LingBot-VLA checkpoint for RoboTwin C2R.
- **Standard LIBERO (Table 2).** Harness VLA (CC) reaches an aggregate 96.0% (384/400): Spatial 97.0, Object 100.0, Goal 94.0, LIBERO-10 93.0. The same frozen π_RLinf checkpoint used inside `VLA_ACT` obtains 95.3%; AtomVLA is 97.0. The controllable primitive interface therefore costs no in-distribution performance.
- **LIBERO-Pro (Table 3; instruction-redirection T and position-swap S, each cell 10 tasks × 10 seeds = 100 trials).** End-to-end VLAs collapse: OpenVLA 0.0, NORA 0.0, π₀ 0.3, MolmoAct 1.5, X-VLA 3.8, AtomVLA 6.3, π₀.₅ 11.0. Coding-agent baselines: Cap-X 18.2, RATS 43.8 (strongest prior). Harness VLA reaches 72.1% with Codex and **82.4% with CC**, improving over RATS by **38.6 percentage points**. The direct π_RLinf baseline reaches 50.0% under the same protocol, showing the gain does not come from the frozen backbone.
- **RoboCasa365 (Table 4).** RLDX-1 reaches a task-weighted overall of 30.0%; Harness VLA reaches 55.4% (Codex) and 48.6% (CC), so the Codex instantiation improves over RLDX-1 by **25.4 percentage points**. Per split, Codex: Atomic-Seen 91.6 / Composite-Seen 56.3 / Composite-Unseen 13.8; CC: 79.4 / 47.5 / 15.0. Baselines: RLDX-1 60.0/21.3/5.0, WorldDreamer 66.3/26.7/9.0, π₀.₅ 39.6/7.1/1.2, π₀ 34.6/6.1/1.1. Bootstrapping uses one reference seed only; evaluation uses ten held-out seeds for Atomic-Seen and five for the Composite splits.
- **Zero-shot LIBERO-Pro GOAL (Table 5; no Task Specific or Global Memory retrieval, 10 seeds/task).** Harness VLA (CC) reaches 31.0% on position-swap (Cap-X 25.6%) and 79.0% on instruction-redirection (Cap-X 16.8%). Contrasting with the few-shot numbers isolates the memory contribution: semantic re-binding largely survives without memory (79.0% zero-shot vs 87.0% few-shot on Goal-T), but spatial perturbation degrades substantially (31.0% vs 87.0% on Goal-S), indicating that spatially perturbed manipulation depends strongly on the task-specific primitive organization discovered during exploration.
- **RoboTwin C2R (Table 6; 50 tasks × 5 randomized seeds, zero-shot clean-to-randomized transfer).** Direct LingBot-VLA reaches 50.4%; Harness VLA raises the same frozen backend to 58.0% (Codex) and **58.4% (CC)**. External baselines: GR00T-N1.7 20.7, π₀.₅ 47.9, StarVLA 10.6.
- **Key Finding 2 / adaptive invocation (Figure 4).** Capping the maximum `VLA_ACT` invocations per episode at k: on LIBERO-Pro, k=1 already gives 52% (above the 50% frozen-policy baseline), k=2 gives 68%, saturating toward the full-harness 82%; on RoboCasa365, k=1 gives 2%, k=3 gives 21%, k=10 gives 39%, with the full harness at 48% (frozen baseline 30%); on RoboTwin C2R, k=1 gives 18%, k=2 gives 42%, k=3 gives 49%, full harness 58% (frozen baseline 50%). A few planner-selected invocations already exceed the frozen policy — VLA calls are sparse, but the ability to re-stage and retry is central.
- **Key Finding 3 / completion attribution (Figure 6).** Fraction of successful rollouts whose final completion predicate fires after an analytic vs a VLA primitive: the LIBERO Pro family finishes mostly analytically (Spatial 72%/28%, Object 80%/20%, Goal 79%/21%, LIBERO-10 84%/16%), whereas RoboCasa365 and RoboTwin end more often inside the VLA primitive (Composite-Seen 40%/60%, Composite-Unseen 17%/83%, Atomic 14%/86%; RoboTwin 50 tasks 34%/66%), consistent with their terminal contact-rich operations such as fixture actuation, constrained placement, and bimanual object interaction.
- **Limitations (authors').** The feedback loop between high-level planner and low-level VLA is open; the system lacks joint fine-tuning via environmental rewards and human preferences (motivating sample-efficient RL such as GRPO); the absence of fine-grained image captioning constrains structural reasoning in highly cluttered, long-horizon tasks. A complementary direction is to combine the fixed-vocabulary composition strategy with automatic skill-discovery systems such as ASPIRE, admitting new reusable skills while retaining the auditable primitive interface and VLA-backed contact specialization.
