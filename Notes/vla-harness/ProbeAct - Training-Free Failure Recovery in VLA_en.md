# ProbeAct: Probe-Guided Training-Free Failure Recovery in Vision-Language-Action Models

> arXiv: 2606.09740 | Year: 2026

## Topic
Training-free VLA failure recovery

## Background
Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, predominantly trained by behavioral cloning on large-scale expert demonstrations, perform strongly on language-conditioned manipulation inside their training distribution but are brittle under realistic out-of-distribution (OOD) perturbations: altered lighting, changed camera viewpoints, modified textures, visual distractors, or slight deviations in initial object configurations can cause success rates to drop precipitously. The authors attribute this fragility to the behavioral cloning objective: rather than grounding actions in the current visual context, the policy bypasses active spatial reasoning and replays a memorized kinematic sequence — a "memory trap." Their own probing experiments localize the bottleneck: the visual backbone still maintains accurate spatial representations of the target object under perturbation, and the collapse occurs exclusively inside the action head, in the projection from features to motor commands.

## Limitations & Research Problem
- **Limitation:** Existing inference-time correction approaches (online corrections, external VLM reasoning modules, visual symbols, explicit 3D reprojection) introduce substantial structural overhead, demand supplementary sensing infrastructure or external reasoning models, and often fully override the VLA's native action stream. Classical recovery approaches — hierarchical TAMP, behavior-tree reactive replanning, recovery RL — require rigorous environment modeling, known failure probabilities, or prohibitively expensive failure-case training data.
- **Problem:** Without modifying pre-trained VLA weights, collecting additional demonstrations, or relying on external 3D sensors and reasoning modules, can grasping and placement failures be detected and recovered at runtime using only the VLA's own internal hidden states as the geometric reference?

## Contributions
- PROBEACT: a lightweight, closed-loop, training-free intervention framework that extracts robust 3D geometric references directly from frozen VLA hidden states, entirely eliminating the structural overhead of external visual sensors.
- An object-agnostic kinematic state machine coupled with a hierarchical Control Barrier Function (CBF) filter, translating relative spatial synchronization into a closed-form action projection that autonomously rescues the policy from catastrophic failure loops while preserving baseline competence.
- Extensive evaluation on LIBERO-plus showing consistent success-rate improvements across diverse visual and spatial perturbation categories, achieved entirely at inference time without VLA weight modification, external reasoning modules, or additional demonstrations — and applicable to both base and fine-tuned VLA policies.
- A quantitative characterization of the OOD failure mechanism: latent spatial information remains intact while the action endpoint has already drifted, providing the empirical foundation for a "read the hidden state, apply a minimal correction" design.

## Methodology
- **Overall architecture.** Three modules run alongside a frozen VLA at inference time: (1) Perception — an internal probe extracting stable 3D object tracks from intermediate LLM activations; (2) Logic — a kinematic state machine detecting physical execution failures via relative robot-object synchronization; (3) Control — a hierarchical CBF filter applying a minimal closed-form projection to the nominal action $a_t$, yielding a safe action $a_t^{cbf}$.
- **Multi-target hidden-state probe.** A multi-head MLP $\phi: \mathbb{R}^{d_{pca}} \to \mathbb{R}^{K\times 3}\times\mathbb{R}^{K}$. Features come from VLA layer 8: the 16×16 spatial tokens are averaged into a 4×4 grid of 4096-dim vectors, then PCA-reduced to $d_{pca}=1024$. The probe is a 4-layer MLP with hidden dimensions [2048, 1024, 512, 256], each layer followed by LayerNorm, ReLU, and dropout ($p=0.1$). Each predicted position carries a sigmoid confidence $c_k$; predictions with $c_k<0.5$ are discarded.
- **Hungarian-matching training and inference-time identity tracking.** Frames contain 1 to $K$ relevant objects, so the probe is trained with a bipartite matching loss (L2 localization + BCE confidence, weighted by $\beta$) minimized over permutations $\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_K$. The output is therefore permutation-invariant, with no temporal consistency in slot indices. At inference, online Hungarian matching along the temporal axis assigns new predictions to existing object tracks by minimizing pairwise Euclidean displacement; a spatial gating threshold bounded by the maximum plausible inter-frame object velocity prevents erroneous ID switching, and unmatched predictions beyond it spawn new tracks.
- **Object-agnostic kinematic state machine.** Failure detection is organized around six mechanical phases (APPROACH, MONITOR, GRASPING, POST GRASP, …) plus a PLACED event. All transitions use object-agnostic signals only: gripper width $q$, end-effector pose $\mathbf{e}=(x_e,y_e,z_e)$, and probe-tracked object positions. Thresholds derive purely from embodiment-specific hardware tolerances (e.g., nominal lift clearance) rather than task-specific tuning.
  - **Grasp verification via kinematic synchronization.** Over a rolling window, relative vertical displacements $\Delta z_e$ and $\Delta z_{obj}$ are computed. A grasp is confirmed only when the gripper stabilizes above its mechanical minimum ($q>\epsilon_{limit}$) and both entities show synchronous positive displacement beyond the hardware noise floor ($\Delta z_e>\tau_{lift}$ and $\Delta z_{obj}>\tau_{track}$), avoiding marking empty-air grasps as successful.
  - **Two grasp failure modes.** Hard empty grasp ($q\le\epsilon_{limit}$, complete closure with nothing between the fingers) and soft empty grasp ($\Delta z_e>\tau_{lift}$ while $\Delta z_{obj}\le\tau_{noise}$, i.e., kinematic decoupling proving the object was not acquired).
  - **Mid-transport drop.** During POST GRASP, an abrupt collapse of gripper width to the mechanical limit while the end-effector is in motion; the drop location is encoded as a CBF zone and a recovery action toward the original grasp target is triggered.
  - **Placement verification.** PLACED fires only when three conditions hold simultaneously: gripper width increases relative to its hold value ($q>q_{hold}+\epsilon_{release}$), the end-effector establishes a diverging retreat trajectory ($\|\mathbf{e}_t-p_{obj}\|>\|\mathbf{e}_{t-1}-p_{obj}\|$), and the held object's coordinates stabilize within the target-location tolerance.
- **Hierarchical CBF filter (two-tier).** On an initial failure the system executes only a stateless push-back, letting the VLA attempt self-correction. Only if the robot fails again in the same spatial region is a memory trap diagnosed and a persistent CBF zone instantiated at the failure coordinate $c$ with safety radius $r_{safe}$: $h(x;c,r_{safe})=\|x-c\|^2-r_{safe}^2$, safe set $\mathcal{H}=\{x:h(x)\ge0\}$. During PRE_GRASP, the nominal translational action $u_{vla}\in\mathbb{R}^3$ is filtered to satisfy the first-order CBF condition $\nabla h(x)^\top u \ge -\gamma h(x)$ via a minimal-intervention QP, giving the closed form $u_{filtered}=u_{vla}+\max\left(0,\frac{-\gamma h(x)-\nabla h(x)^\top u_{vla}}{\|\nabla h(x)\|_2^2}\right)\nabla h(x)$ with $\nabla h(x)=2(x-c)$. If the proposed action already respects the boundary, the projection evaluates to zero and the filter is an exact identity map, preserving baseline competence. Zones are dynamically flushed when the end-effector enters the terminal proximity $\rho_{close}$ of the true target or upon PLACED, and the filter is intentionally bypassed during GRASPING and TRANSPORTING to permit unhindered physical interaction.
- **Multi-step task support.** For tasks such as "put both moka pots on the stove," task language is parsed for keywords (both, two, and) and decomposed into $N$ (pick, place) subtask pairs. After PLACED, the system blacklists the just-placed object for target matching, resets the probe tracker, clears all CBF zones, and re-enters PRE_GRASP — isolating failure modes across subtasks while reusing the same detection infrastructure.

## Experiments & Key Numbers
- **Setup.** Backbone: OpenVLA-OFT. Benchmark: LIBERO-plus, spanning seven perturbation categories (Camera Viewpoints, Robot Initial States, Language Instructions, Lighting condition, Background Textures, Sensor Noise, Objects Layout), scored by LIBERO's built-in goal predicate. Probe training: 50,000 (hidden-state, object-positions) pairs collected by rolling out the baseline VLA, with object positions from the simulator's `obj_of_interest` list; 200 epochs, AdamW, batch 512, cosine LR schedule. All experiments run on two NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000 (Blackwell) GPUs.
- **Main results (Table 1, success rate %).** PROBEACT reaches **74.1** overall — the highest among all evaluated VLAs — versus 69.6 for the OpenVLA-OFT baseline (+4.5). Per category, PROBEACT vs OpenVLA-OFT: Camera 63.8 vs 56.4, Robot 40.3 vs 31.9, Language 82.0 vs 79.5, Light 93.6 vs 88.7, Background 93.5 vs 93.3, Noise 76.8 vs 75.8, Layout 80.9 vs 74.2. Other baselines (total): OpenVLA 15.6, NORA 39.0, WorldVLA 25.0, UniVLA 43.9, $\pi_0$ 53.6, $\pi_0$-Fast 61.6, RIPT-VLA 68.4.
- **Gains concentrate under geometric distribution shift.** The largest improvements occur on Robot Initial States (+8.4) and Camera Viewpoints (+7.4), consistent with the memory-trap / "Phantom Grasp" hypothesis: the policy identifies the correct target but its motor execution spatially collapses to a memorized training mean.
- **Generalization to fine-tuned VLAs (Table 2, Robot Initial States category, success rate %).** On OpenVLA-OFT-mixdata — a public checkpoint fine-tuned by mixing original LIBERO data with LIBERO-plus perturbations — PROBEACT improves all four LIBERO suites: Spatial 30.6→32.6 (+2.0), Object 23.9→30.7 (+6.8), Goal 20.8→25.7 (+4.9), 10: 36.6→39.7 (+3.1), average 28.0→32.2. Mixdata-style training therefore mitigates but does not eliminate memorization-induced execution failures, and PROBEACT is orthogonal to data-side remedies rather than a substitute.
- **Action-output drift analysis (Table 3, 300 LIBERO-plus episodes under Objects Layout perturbations, same VLA forward pass).** Hidden-state probe error vs action-endpoint error against the ground-truth object position: All 6.9 cm vs 23.6 cm; Success 3.4 cm vs 7.8 cm; Failure 10.4 cm vs 34.9 cm. On failed episodes the action endpoint drifts to 34.9 cm from ground truth while the probe stays around 10 cm — the spatial information needed for a correct grasp is still present in the intermediate representation; what fails is the projection from features to motor commands. (The running text quotes "12.4 cm" for the probe error, inconsistent with the 10.4 cm in Table 3 — an apparent typo in the original.)
- **Probe training data selection (Table 4, 3D position regression $R^2$).** Four pooling schemes (img-spatial, img-mean, last token, lang-mean) across layers 8/12/16/20/24/28. Spatial pooling dominates at every layer, and spatial information peaks at shallow-mid layers and decays in deeper ones. The global best is **layer 8 with image-spatial pooling, $R^2=0.968$** (same layer: 0.926 / 0.815 / 0.869 for the other schemes; img-spatial falls to 0.934 at layer 28). This configuration is adopted as the default throughout.
- **Step efficiency (Table 5, libero-plus goal, 2,591 tasks total).** On the 1,643 tasks both methods complete, PROBEACT uses 120 steps vs the baseline's 114 — only 6 extra steps (~5% overhead). On the 151 tasks PROBEACT rescues from baseline failure, recovery completes in 197 steps on average versus the baseline's 600-step timeout (−403 steps). On the 724 tasks where both fail, step counts are identical at 600. Across the full benchmark PROBEACT averages *fewer* steps than the baseline (255 vs 275, −20). Figure 1 reports probe runtime of <3 ms per call.
- **Limitations.** Validation is currently confined to a simulated benchmark with idealized rigid-body dynamics. Sim-to-real transferability of the kinematic state machine remains untested: physical hardware introduces variable contact friction, soft-body deformation, and high-frequency encoder noise, and verifying that the kinematic synchronization signals stay robust under real contact dynamics and sensor noise is an essential prerequisite for physical deployment.
