# StarVLA: A Lego-like Codebase for Vision-Language-Action Model Developing

> arXiv: 2604.05014 | Year: 2026

## Topic
Modular unified VLA codebase

## Background
Building generalist embodied agents requires jointly integrating perception, language understanding, and action generation — the core capability targeted by Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. Existing approaches fall into two broad families: *VLM-based methods*, which repurpose a language model's representational capacity for action decoding, and *world-model-based methods*, which employ generative architectures to jointly model action distributions and future observations. These two directions have largely developed in isolation, with incompatible codebases, interface assumptions, and evaluation protocols — a situation the authors describe as a "Tower of Babel" for VLA research, in which principled comparison, reproduction, and recombination of ideas are all impractical.

## Limitations & Research Problem
- **Limitation:** Fragmentation occurs at three levels. At the *architecture* level, action-decoding designs vary widely (autoregressive tokenization, parallel regression, diffusion, flow matching), making systematic cross-paradigm comparison difficult. At the *system* level, methods ship with tightly coupled assumptions on model architecture, data processing, and training pipelines, preventing component reuse across projects. At the *evaluation* level, results are reported on disjoint benchmark subsets under inconsistent protocols, making fair comparison infeasible. The authors attribute this to the *lack of a unified abstraction for VLA systems*.
- **Limitation:** Existing open-source codebases (e.g., those released with GR00T N1 and π0) are largely method-specific and do not support (i) modular composition across different action-decoding paradigms, (ii) reusable training across heterogeneous data sources, or (iii) standardized evaluation and deployment across benchmarks and embodiments.
- **Problem:** Is there a single abstraction under which VLM-based and world-model-based VLA methods can share one data pipeline, training loop, and evaluation protocol — so that backbone and action head can each be swapped independently, enabling controlled comparison of individual design choices?

## Contributions
- **StarVLA**, an open-source VLA research platform built on a **backbone–action head decomposition**: a shared vision-language backbone encodes the scene and instruction, and a pluggable action head maps the resulting representation to motor commands. Because both the outer system boundary (raw observations → actions) and the inner backbone–head boundary are governed by standardized contracts, StarVLA achieves **bidirectional modularity** — either component can be replaced without affecting the other or the surrounding infrastructure.
- Four representative paradigms implemented under this shared abstraction: **StarVLA-FAST** (FAST tokenizer, autoregressive discrete action tokens in the LLM vocabulary), **StarVLA-OFT** (lightweight MLP regressing continuous actions in parallel with L1 loss), **StarVLA-π** (layer-wise cross-DiT flow-matching action expert with iterative denoising), and **StarVLA-GR00T** (dual-system design: VL backbone as System 2 slow reasoning, DiT flow-matching module as System 1 fast action generation). Both VLM backbones (Qwen3-VL) and world-model backbones (Cosmos-Predict2) are supported as drop-in alternatives.
- Cross-embodiment learning and multimodal co-training are treated as **reusable, paradigm-agnostic training configurations** rather than method-specific add-ons; every recipe applies uniformly across all supported paradigms.
- Integration of mainstream benchmarks (LIBERO, SimplerEnv, RoboTwin 2.0, RoboCasa-GR1, BEHAVIOR-1K, and others — Table 1 reports 7 integrated simulation benchmarks) through a unified server–client testing interface that supports both simulation evaluation and real-robot deployment without code changes.
- A **generalized VLA perspective**: empirically, VLM-based and world-model-based methods are better understood not as fundamentally distinct paradigms but as variations within a common structural framework, differing mainly in the form of the auxiliary learning signal (language-aligned reasoning vs. future observation prediction).

## Methodology
- **Unified policy formulation:** a VLA system is modeled as π(a_{t:t+k}, y_aux | x_≤t, ℓ), where x_≤t is the multimodal observation history (visual, depth, tactile, proprioceptive), ℓ the language instruction, a_{t:t+k} the predicted k-step action chunk, and y_aux optional auxiliary outputs. Training decomposes as L = L_action + L_aux. Direct VLA modeling sets L_aux = 0; VLM-based VLA uses language-aligned auxiliary objectives (sub-task planning, spatial grounding, structured reasoning); WM-based VLA uses future observation prediction.
- **Unified I/O interface:** all framework modules inherit from a common base class exposing `forward({raw images, str, ...}) → loss dict` (training entry) and `predict_action({raw images, str, ...}) → {normalized_actions, ...}` (inference entry). Training inputs mirror the raw observations a robot receives at deployment rather than heavily preprocessed dataloader tensors, minimizing train/test distribution mismatch — codified as a *deployment-time invariant* first-class contract.
- **Compositional framework:** every method decomposes into a VL backbone plus a pluggable action head, assembled declaratively via YAML (load backbone, then attach action head).
- **Training paradigms:** (1) robot-only SFT (`train_starvla.py`; full-parameter fine-tuning or selective freezing via `trainer.freeze_modules`, multiple parameter groups with distinct learning rates, bfloat16 autocast, gradient accumulation/clipping, cosine schedule with minimum LR); (2) multi-objective co-training (`train_starvla_cotrain.py`; two dataloaders, two forward/backward passes per optimization step, VLM loss scaled by `trainer.loss_scale.vlm`); (3) cross-embodiment co-training via `datasets.vla_data.data_mix` specifying (dataset, sampling weight, robot type) tuples materialized as a `LeRobotMixtureDataset`; (4) RL fine-tuning is planned in collaboration with the RLinf project but not yet in the public codebase. Distributed execution uses explicit PyTorch loops on Accelerate + DeepSpeed.
- **Evaluation and deployment:** a checkpoint is loaded via `baseframework.from_pretrained()` and hosted as a lightweight WebSocket policy server; the benchmark evaluator may run in a separate conda environment with its own simulator dependencies and interact through a thin msgpack client. Benchmark differences are isolated in small adapter files (`model2libero_interface.py`, `model2simpler_interface.py`, `model2robotwin_interface.py`) handling image resizing, action unnormalization from `dataset_statistics.json`, action ensembling, sticky grippers, and delta/relative-to-absolute conversion. For real robots, the controller plays the client role while the model service remains unchanged.
- **Generalist setting:** one model jointly trained on the merged training sets of LIBERO, SimplerEnv, RoboTwin 2.0, and RoboCasa-GR1, with learning rate 1×10⁻⁴, total batch size 256, no task-specific action heads, and a unified padding strategy expanding lower-DoF actions to a shared 32-dimensional action vector.

## Experiments & Key Numbers
- **LIBERO** (4 suites, 10 tasks × 50 episodes = 500 trials per suite; 8×A100, per-device batch 16): with a Qwen3-VL-4B backbone and only 30K steps (9.54 epochs), StarVLA reaches average success rates of 95.4 (FAST), 96.6 (OFT), 95.7 (π), and 96.5 (GR00T). OpenVLA-OFT requires 175K steps (223 epochs) to reach 97.1, so StarVLA-OFT attains 96.6 with **6× fewer steps and 23× fewer epochs**; π0+FAST scores 85.5 and GR00T-N1.5 (20K steps) 86.5. Swapping the backbone to Cosmos-Predict2-2B yields comparable results (OFT 95.8, π 95.5, GR00T 95.2 — all ≥95.2), showing the abstraction generalizes across VL backbones.
- **SimplerEnv** (16×A100; each reported setting rerun with the full official evaluation five times and averaged): on WidowX Visual Matching, Qwen3-VL-4B reaches up to 65.3% average (StarVLA-GR00T, 20K steps) and Cosmos-Predict2-2B up to 61.6%, versus π0 27.1, π0-FAST 48.3, GR00T-N1.5 61.9, SpatialVLA 42.7, Magma 35.8. On Google Robot, StarVLA-OFT achieves 76.0 average under Visual Matching (previous best CogACT 74.8) and 70.2 under Variant Aggregation (SpatialVLA 70.7).
- **RoboCasa-GR1** (24 tasks, one jointly trained policy, 50 rollouts per task; 250-rollout reporting protocol): StarVLA-OFT is best at 48.8%, followed by StarVLA-GR00T 47.8%, StarVLA-π 43.9%, and the discrete StarVLA-FAST at only 39.0%; baselines are π0.5 37.0% and GR00T-N1.6 47.6%. StarVLA-OFT thus outperforms π0.5 by 11.8 points, and the 39.0 → 48.8 spread shows that action-head choice matters far more on this harder benchmark than on LIBERO or SimplerEnv.
- **RoboTwin 2.0** (50 tasks × 2 setups × 100 episodes = 10,000 trials; 48×A100, 150K steps): clean/randomized success rates are 88.0/88.5 (GR00T), 88.2/88.3 (OFT), 88.1/88.8 (π), and 72.5/83.2 (FAST), versus π0 65.9/58.4, π0.5 82.7/76.8, X-VLA 72.9/72.8, and LingBot-VLA 88.6/86.7.
- **Multimodal co-training** (ST4VLA study built on StarVLA): action-only fine-tuning collapses RefCOCO-g grounding to near-random within 20K steps. Vanilla co-training already improves manipulation by +4.1% Google Robot VM and +6.4% WidowX over vanilla VLA while recovering multimodal capability; the spatially pretrained variant reaches 84.6/75.9 on Google Robot VM/VA and 73.2 on WidowX while preserving strong spatial grounding (RefCOCO-g IoU@0.5 = 71.2, RoboRefIt Acc@0.5 = 74.3) and multimodal understanding (MME 1411, MMVet 23.3, TextVQA 28.6, POPE 86.2). The vanilla VLA baseline scores 66.1/63.5 on Google Robot and 54.7 on WidowX.
- **Generalist (one model jointly trained across benchmarks):** LIBERO average 97.8, WidowX 70.2, Google VA 73.8, Google VM 79.3, RoboTwin clean* 88.7 / random* 87.8, and RoboCasa-GR1 57.3 — improving RoboCasa-GR1 from the best specialist average of 48.8% to 57.3% while remaining competitive elsewhere.
- **Computation efficiency** (StarVLA-GR00T with Qwen3-VL-4B on RoboCasa-GR1, A100 80GB, wall-clock per 100K optimization steps): on a single 8×A100 node, increasing per-GPU batch from 2 to 24 moves step latency 0.703 → 2.404 s/step (3.4×), sample throughput 22.7 → 79.9 samples/s, and GPU utilization 74% → 96%. Multi-node with per-GPU batch fixed at 8: 8 GPUs at 0.735 s/step and 87.0 samples/s; 32 GPUs at 0.899 s/step and 284.7 samples/s (81.9% scaling efficiency); 256 GPUs at 0.931 s/step and 2200.0 samples/s with 79.1% scaling efficiency. Inter-node communication adds a one-time latency overhead (0.735 → ~0.93 s/step), after which parallel efficiency stabilizes at 79–80% beyond 32 GPUs.
- **Scale of integrated benchmarks:** LIBERO — 130 tasks, 50 demonstrations per task, ~6.5K trajectories; LIBERO-Plus — a test-only set of 10,030 tasks spanning 7 perturbation factors and 21 low-level components; RoboCasa-GR1 — 24 tasks, ~1,000 demonstrations each, ~24K trajectories; RoboTwin 2.0 — 50 tasks with 50 clean plus 500 randomized demonstrations each (~550 per task, 27.5K total); BEHAVIOR-1K — 1,000 everyday activities across 50 interactive scenes with more than 9,000 objects, with the BEHAVIOR Challenge releasing 10,000 teleoperated demonstrations (over 1,200 hours) for 50 household tasks (200 per task); CALVIN — 4 environments (A, B, C, D) and 34 manipulation tasks, evaluated under ABC→D with 1,000 task sequences of length 5.
