# VLA-Corrector: Lightweight Detect-and-Correct Inference for Adaptive Action Horizon

> arXiv: 2607.01804 | Year: 2026

## Topic
Adaptive action horizon via detect-and-correct

## Background
Modern generative VLA policies adopt an action chunk mechanism: a single forward pass predicts a sequence of future actions, of which the first *H* are executed open-loop within a fixed action horizon, amortizing policy-call cost and preserving temporal smoothness. This "predict-then-blindly-execute" paradigm sacrifices closed-loop reactivity: fresh observations keep arriving during the horizon but are ignored, creating an open-loop blind spot. In contact-rich manipulation, small local perturbations are rapidly amplified into compounding errors and eventual task failure. The authors quantify this performance–efficiency trade-off across π0.5, SmolVLA, and X-VLA: for π0.5, increasing the horizon reduces policy calls by about 4× but lowers success from roughly 64% to below 49%.

## Limitations & Research Problem
- **Limitation:** A fixed action horizon forces a choice between two extremes — *H*=1 is fully closed-loop but requires a full VLA inference at every control step, negating the efficiency gains chunking was designed for, while large *H* amortizes computation but widens the blind spot in which errors silently accumulate. Since the optimal horizon depends on task difficulty, environment dynamics, and sim-to-real mismatch, no single static value remains optimal across scenarios.
- **Problem:** (1) How to **detect execution deviations in time** and **terminate stale actions** before errors compound beyond recovery; (2) how to **correct the trajectory after truncation** — naive replanning is often insufficient, since the VLA may regenerate actions that still fail to escape the deviated state, leaving the robot trapped again.

## Contributions
- Systematically quantifies the performance–efficiency trade-off induced by fixed action horizons in action-chunked VLA policies, showing the open-loop blind spot consistently degrades robustness across backbones.
- Proposes VLA-Corrector, a lightweight inference-time framework that augments a **frozen** VLA backbone with latent-space monitoring, event-triggered truncation, and recovery-oriented guided re-inference, naturally inducing an event-triggered **adaptive action horizon**.
- Validates the method in simulation and on a real robot, demonstrating stronger robustness, higher success-per-call efficiency, and adaptive correction behavior, with cross-architecture transferability.

## Methodology
- **Training the external latent dynamics corrector:** The VLA backbone is first fine-tuned on the benchmark training set, then frozen; its visual encoder *E* extracts latents. For a transition (o_t, a_t, o_{t+k}), the target residual is ΔZ*_{t+k} = Z^real_{t+k} − Z^real_t, and a lightweight module M_φ is trained to predict ΔẐ_{t+k} = M_φ(Z^real_t, a_t). Predicting a **residual** rather than an absolute future state suppresses static scene content and focuses on task-relevant dynamics. The objective combines magnitude matching and directional consistency: L_corr = ‖ΔẐ − ΔZ*‖² + β[1 − CosSim(ΔẐ, ΔZ*)]. In practice a 40M MLP suffices.
- **Latent-space Vision Monitor (LVM) for online detection:** Although the policy is not re-queried during execution, fresh observations remain available. LVM computes the actual residual ΔZ^real_{t+k} and the expected residual ΔZ^exp_{t+k}, and forms the inconsistency score E_t = 1 − CosSim(ΔZ^exp_{t+k}, ΔZ^real_{t+k}) as a continuous visual-dynamics-mismatch signal.
- **Event-triggered truncation:** Thresholding E_t directly is unstable because transient outliers cause false triggers. Instead, a sliding window of recent scores yields a median M_e and MAD, defining two adaptive thresholds T_on = M_e + λ_on·MAD and T_off = M_e + λ_off·MAD (λ_on > λ_off, providing hysteresis). An interrupt fires only when E_t > T_on holds for *p* consecutive steps; isolated spikes are ignored. Upon interrupt, the remaining queued actions are discarded and the realized horizon becomes H_adaptive = h < H.
- **Online Gradient Guidance (OGG) for corrective inference:** Applied only to the single policy call immediately following an interrupt. At flow-matching denoising step τ, the velocity field v_τ gives the estimated clean chunk Â_0 = A^τ − τ·v_τ, whose first action â_t has predicted latent effect ΔẐ_act = M_φ(Z^real_t, â_t). The corrective direction is ΔZ_corr = ΔZ_exp − ΔZ_dev, where ΔZ_dev = Z^real_t − Z^real_{t−k} is the accumulated deviation since the last stable step t−k and ΔZ_exp is the residual predicted from t−k. The guidance loss L_OGG = 1 − CosSim(ΔẐ_act, ΔZ_corr) is injected into the velocity field: v^guide_τ = v_τ − η∇_{v_τ}L_OGG. Because OGG modifies the velocity field rather than perturbing action coordinates directly, it remains compatible with the original flow-matching process and yields smoother corrective replanning.

## Experiments & Key Numbers
- **Setup:** MetaWorld, LIBERO, and a real AgileX PiPER 6-DoF arm; π0.5 as the main backbone, with SmolVLA and X-VLA for cross-architecture evaluation.
- **Cross-architecture on MetaWorld (Table 1, avg. success %):** π0.5 48.70 → 64.35 (+15.65); SmolVLA 61.90 → 66.65 (+4.75); X-VLA 55.55 → 59.60 (+4.05). The strongest gain is on the Very Hard split of π0.5: 41.0 → 65.0 (+24.0).
- **Sample efficiency on LIBERO (Table 2):** Fully fine-tuned π0.5 averages 96.95; few-shot fine-tuned 94.00; few-shot + VLA-Corrector reaches **97.80** (+3.80), **surpassing the fully fine-tuned baseline**. The Long split improves 86.6 → 93.4 (+6.8).
- **Success-per-call efficiency (Table 4):** Largest relative efficiency gains are +29.9% for π0.5, +45.3% for SmolVLA, and +39.1% for X-VLA. π0.5 at horizon 50: success 48.72 → 58.70 with average policy calls 5.15 → 4.98 (+24.6%). SmolVLA at horizon 10: 61.90 → 73.00 with calls 19.27 → 15.64 (+45.3%). Gains grow at longer horizons.
- **Corrector data efficiency (Table 3, horizon 50):** Baseline 48.72; r=0.2 → 48.32 (−0.40), r=0.4 → 49.15 (+0.43), r=0.6 → 52.20 (+3.48), r=0.8 → 52.26 (+3.54), r=1.0 → 54.32 (+5.60), saturating around r = 0.6–0.8 with clear diminishing returns.
- **Mechanism analysis:** 83.7% of truncations occur in critical phases (precise grasping, alignment) versus 16.3% in non-critical phases — 5.1× more frequent in critical phases. Failed episodes show a heavier high-E_t tail and trigger more interrupts than successful ones. OGG improves post-interrupt recovery at every difficulty level (Easy +0.28, Medium +0.13, Hard +0.22, Very Hard +0.30, Overall +0.23; recovery counted when E_t < T_off within 10 steps of the interrupt).
- **Real world (Table 5, AgileX PiPER; 3 task groups × 3 tasks × 20 trials, 95% binomial CIs):** Pick-place 70.0±11.6 → 78.3±10.4 (+8.3); Alignment 56.7±12.5 → 73.3±11.2 (+16.6); Disturbance recovery 40.0±12.4 → 68.3±11.8 (+28.3); average 55.6±7.3 → 73.3±6.5 (+17.7). The benefit is largest under online disturbances.
- **Ablations (Tables 6/7):** Truncation alone raises average success from 48.70 to 60.35 (+11.65); adding OGG reaches 64.35 (+15.65). On detector design, the decoupled external LVM + OGG (64.35) clearly outperforms a coupled auxiliary head fine-tuned into the backbone (49.55, +14.80), suggesting the internal auxiliary objective perturbs representations also used for VLM-to-action planning. Sensitivity analysis indicates a moderate guidance strength and a 40M LVM are sufficient; scaling the monitor beyond 40M brings little additional gain.
