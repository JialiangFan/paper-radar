# RePO-VLA: Recovery-Driven Policy Optimization for Vision-Language-Action Model

> arXiv: 2605.09410 | Year: 2026

## Topic
Recovery-driven VLA policy optimization

## Background
Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models remain brittle in long-horizon, contact-rich bimanual manipulation: small deviations in grasp pose, object contact, or inter-arm timing quickly compound into execution drift. Such perturbations rarely make a task impossible; instead they push the system into recoverable adverse states that require targeted correction before nominal progress can resume. Yet mainstream VLA training still relies on supervised fine-tuning (SFT) over successful demonstrations and discards failed rollouts wholesale, leaving the drift-then-correct temporal structure essentially unsupervised.

## Limitations & Research Problem
- **Limitation:** Success-only imitation creates a failure-utilization blind spot. Early phases of failed rollouts contain valid approach, contact preparation, and partial-manipulation priors, but are discarded together with the terminal breakdown. Conversely, naively imitating an entire recovery demonstration conflates the distribution that *causes* drift with the distribution that *repairs* it, producing causal confusion and mode averaging (the policy treats the preceding error as a necessary trigger for recovery). Sparse binary rewards further fail to distinguish useful approach, temporary stagnation, active recovery, and terminal failure.
- **Limitation:** Existing recovery mechanisms mostly live outside the policy — VLM/LLM replanning, interactive correction, online failure detectors, or hand-coded retry rules — and are disconnected from low-level contact dynamics. Methods that synthesize recovery data (e.g., FailSafe) still train with SFT and do not separate erroneous states from corrective actions. Evaluation is likewise missing a benchmark that decouples nominal competence from corrective capability.
- **Problem:** How can a single VLA policy exploit mixed-quality success / recovery / failure trajectories according to their distinct semantic roles, so that it autonomously recovers from adverse states without online failure detectors or heuristic retries?

## Contributions
- **RePO-VLA**, a two-phase (RAI + VCR) recovery-driven policy optimization framework that assigns distinct roles to success, recovery, and failure trajectories: progress alignment preserves useful failure prefixes, low-value labels expose drift-to-correction boundaries, and a data engine converts adverse states into corrective rollouts for value-conditioned refinement.
- **PAS-VF**, a Progress-Aware Semantic Value Function built on frozen dual-tower encoders plus lightweight adapters, which assigns dense, self-referential progress labels to mixed-quality data without manual progress annotation.
- **FRBench**, a recovery benchmark with standardized error injection (E1–E4), a phase-based protocol (Nominal / Error / Recovery) across RoboTwin simulation and real-world bimanual tasks, in which recovery scoring is conditioned on a *verified* adverse state so that better reaching or grasping is not mistaken for true correction.
- **Empirical validation** showing strong recovery gains over SFT baselines and an empirical scaling trend with increasing recovery-data density.

## Methodology
- **Phase I — Recovery-Aware Initialization (RAI):** Trajectory Slicing with History Reset (**TSHR**) cuts a raw recovery trajectory at `t_rec`, discards the failure prefix, and extracts `τ' = {(o_t, a_t)}_{t=t_rec}^{T}` as an independent correction episode; the observation-history buffer is cleared at the first recovery frame and refills normally, yielding `D_rec^reset`. This forces recovery to be learned as a **state-conditioned skill** rather than a replay of a particular failed rollout. Training is a weighted SFT over expert and reset recovery data (Eq. 1, balanced by λ). TSHR is used only in RAI; VCR and deployment use the standard rolling buffer.
- **PAS-VF (progress-aware semantic value):** A frozen V-JEPA spatiotemporal encoder `E_v` and a frozen text encoder `E_t` project trajectories and language instructions into a shared manifold `Z`; only vision/text adapters are trained. Training performs **monotonic progress alignment** on successful trajectories, regressing `CosSim(z_t^v, z^l)` for prefix `τ_{0:t}` onto normalized temporal progress `t/T_τ` (Eq. 2). Inference performs **self-referential progress estimation**: for an unlabeled failure, `V(τ_fail) = max_{z∈C_ref} CosSim(z^v_{τ_fail}, z)` against a reference cluster of successful embeddings (Eq. 3), which avoids manual annotation and keeps the score task-relative.
- **Progress-Aware Hindsight Labeling:** Each frame in the raw mixed dataset receives a dense label `v_t ∈ [0,1]`. Successful trajectories and effective recovery suffixes get `v_t = 1.0`; deviating recovery prefixes (error segments) get `v_t = 0.0`, explicitly telling the policy that actions leading *into* the adverse state must not be averaged with the corrective actions that follow under the same raw history; pure failures keep early kinematic priors via **reliability decay** `v_t = V(τ_fail)·(1 - t/T)^α` (Eq. 4) with **α = 3.0**, retaining useful early motion while sharply down-weighting states near irreversible failure.
- **Phase II — Value-Conditioned Refinement (VCR):** The flow-matching **π₀.₅** policy is augmented with a value token `e_val = MLP_val(v_t)` attended jointly with visual, language, and history tokens, then fine-tuned on `D_total = D_succ ∪ D_rec^raw ∪ D_fail` (Eq. 5). VCR uses the *unmodified* rolling history `H_t^raw`, so value labels — not history truncation — disambiguate low-value drift from high-value correction, matching the history distribution seen at deployment.
- **Deployment (goal-conditioned autonomous recovery):** No online estimation of `V(τ)`, no history clearing, no failure detector. The policy runs with the standard rolling buffer `H_t^roll` and a fixed target value `v = 1.0` at every step, using the learned success manifold as an attractor; when low-value observations remain in the buffer, the high-value token selects the corrective branch learned during VCR.
- **Failure-Recovery Data Engine (two complementary sources):** (a) **Interception-based synthetic injection** hooks expert-planner execution nodes and injects E1–E4 errors during critical segments (grasp, lift), after which a hierarchical planner restores the task state — a five-step sequence: expert oracle computes `τ_nom`; the engine monitors and intercepts a critical node; an E1–E4 perturbation overrides the nominal action; the perturbed execution projects the robot into an adverse state; a hierarchical recovery planner synthesizes corrective actions. (b) **Policy-induced rollouts** collect closed-loop failures from trained base policies with expert-planner intervention, yielding in-distribution, model-specific recovery data that captures the compounding execution drift synthetic errors cannot reproduce. Both streams merge into `D_rec^raw` for VCR; RAI derives `D_rec^reset` from them via TSHR.
- **FRBench perturbation taxonomy** (nominal action `a_t = [p_t, R_t, g_t]`): **E1** `premature_close` — gripper closes early during approach, `g'_t = g_closed` for `t ∈ [t_approach, t_approach + N_hold]`; **E2** `grasp_slip` — gripper opens during lift for a deterministic window `t ∈ [t_lift, t_lift + 30]`; **E3** `grasp_position_offset` — cm-level translational offset `p'_t = p_t + δp_trans`, `δp_trans ~ U(-d, d)`; **E4** `grasp_orientation_mismatch` — large rotational mismatch with lateral stabilization, `R'_t = R_t · ΔR_err`, `p'_t = p_t + δp_lat`. In simulation, failure is triggered when execution exceeds the maximum nominal success duration: `F_t = I(t > T_max)`, `T_max = max_i(T_succ^i)`; in the real world, recovery episodes are curated by human teleoperators who intentionally induce adverse states and demonstrate corrections.

## Experiments & Key Numbers
- **FRBench-Sim scale (Table 2):** 23,453 generated bimanual manipulation episodes over 46 distinct tasks and 2 environment modes (clean / randomized); 17,061 nominal successes and 6,392 verified multi-step failure-recovery trajectories. Error-type coverage: E1 Premature Close 8,022, E2 Grasp Slip 3,516, E3 Position Offset 4,686, E4 Orientation Mismatch 688 (counts exceed 6,392 because of combinatorial testing across environments before final sequence filtering).
- **Simulation protocol:** 10 RoboTwin tasks, 50 rollouts per task; recovery trials inject Dynamic Grasp Failure by holding the gripper open for 30 frames (~1 s) at grasp initiation. Baselines: RDT, GO-1, π₀, π₀.₅, and Phase I ablations.
- **Nominal robustness (Table 3):** Phase I (w/o Fail) averages 44.6 clean → 44.0 randomized (+17.2 / +10.4 over π₀.₅), remaining essentially stable under randomization, whereas π₀ drops from 33.9 to 12.9 — recovery-aware initialization does not trade away base task competence. π₀.₅ itself scores 27.4 / 33.6; RDT 22.5 / 8.8; GO-1 28.1 / 26.0.
- **Injected-failure results (Table 3):** π₀.₅ averages 15.0 clean / 15.4 randomized; full RePO-VLA reaches **37.0 clean (+22.0) and 43.0 randomized (+27.6)**. Gains are largest on tasks that force a genuine re-grasp or re-alignment (e.g., Blocks RGB 20/22 → 70/80, Blocks Size 2/6 → 60/40, Place Bread 0/0 → 90/60).
- **Real-world setup:** Two Dobot X-Trainer arms; tasks Pour Water, Cook Vegetable, Tidy Desk, Fold Towel; 200 expert demonstrations and 50 teleoperated recovery episodes per task; 10 trials per task per condition. In the adversarial setting a human injects dynamic displacement, kinematic interference, or forced slippage at critical phases.
- **Real-world main results (Table 4):** Standard averages — π₀ 27.5, π₀.₅ 25.0, Phase I **42.5**, Full (1x) 40.0 (+15.0 over π₀.₅). Adversarial averages — π₀ 12.5, π₀.₅ 20.0, Phase I **37.5**, Full (1x) 30.0 (+10.0). Full (1x) does not yet dominate Phase I, revealing a data-density bottleneck: value-conditioned refinement must model a more diverse distribution of natural errors, and sparse recovery data makes the value landscape noisy.
- **Recovery-data scaling (Table 5 / Fig. 6):** Real-world recovery data scaled 1x → 2x → 4x on Pour Water and Fold Towel. Standard averages: π₀ 40, π₀.₅ 35, Phase I 50, Full (1x) 40, Full* (2x) 70, **Full** (4x) 80** (+45 over π₀.₅, +30 over Phase I). Adversarial averages: π₀ 20, π₀.₅ 20, Phase I 40, Full (1x) 30, Full* (2x) 65, **Full** (4x) 75** (+55 over π₀.₅, +35 over Phase I). Per task under adversarial perturbation, Pour Water reaches 80 (+60 over π₀.₅) and Fold Towel 70 (+50). The abstract summarizes this as raising adversarial success from 20% to 75% on average and up to 80% in scaled real-world trials.
- **Ablations (Fig. 7):** (a) Raw failure-recovery data without history reset causes causal confusion, while TSHR restores recovery; a heuristic-retry baseline trained on the same recovery data remains weaker than value guidance, indicating the key benefit is conditioning the action distribution toward high-progress regions rather than merely retrying. (b) In a 30-trial validation, enabling `v = 1` consistently improves success — Pour Water 76.7% and Fold Towel 73.3% (versus roughly 50% and 46.7% with `v = 0`) — evidence that value conditioning is a stable deployment-time control signal, not a fragile one-off prompt. (c) A decay sweep identifies **α = 3** as the best trade-off: α = 1 under-penalizes near-terminal failures, α = 10 discards useful early approach behavior.
- **Qualitative visualization (Sec. 6):** Recovery samples generated in RoboTwin with the ALOHA-Agilex embodiment; for each of the four core error types (E1–E4), six task examples are shown across both clean and domain-randomized environments. Each sequence uses 9 keyframes sampled from a single episode — frames 1–3 the Error Attempt phase (physics-driven perturbations rather than simple Gaussian noise), frames 4–8 the Recovery phase (planner-based trajectories with re-perception and corrective actions), frame 9 the resumed Normal execution from the corrected state.
