# What Frozen VLAs Already Know About Success: A Probing Study of Value-Like Structure in Foundation Robot Policies

> arXiv: 2605.28527 | Year: 2026

## Topic
Probing value-like structure in VLAs

## Background
Vision–language–action (VLA) policies are trained purely to imitate actions; their loss never asks them to estimate reward, progress, or eventual success. Yet the rollouts collected from such policies in deployment — successful and failed, together with their outcome labels — inherently contain that information. This paper asks whether outcome-relevant structure survives inside the *frozen* representation in a form a linear probe can recover, and whether that same readout can alter which action the policy takes, without any policy update or separately trained reward model.

## Limitations & Research Problem
- **Limitation:** Recent robot foundation policies (RT-1/RT-2, PaLM-E, Open X-Embodiment, OpenVLA, OpenVLA-OFT, Octo, SmolVLA, π₀, π₀.₅) are all trained to imitate actions; none is trained to estimate reward, progress, or future success. The representational consequences of that asymmetry remain underexplored. Meanwhile, test-time candidate selection (Visual Foresight, Diffusion Policy and related receding-horizon methods) typically relies on an *external* reward, cost, or world model rather than on the policy's own features.
- **Limitation:** A well-fitting offline probe is not by itself evidence of value-like content. Manipulation data are full of surface-level shortcuts: tasks have different baseline success rates (task-identity shortcut), timestep correlates with progress (elapsed-time shortcut), and successful vs. failed rollouts differ in coarse visual statistics. Prior probing work rarely removes these confounds explicitly.
- **Problem:** Does a value-like signal genuinely exist in frozen VLA representations; does it survive a stringent same-task, same-timestep matched control; and is it behaviourally usable at test time?

## Contributions
- **Decodability across backbones.** Outcome-derived value-like targets are linearly decodable at comparable strength from four different representation families — autoregressive VLA (OpenVLA, OpenVLA-OFT, SmolVLA), VLA-distilled VLM (π₀.₅), self-supervised vision (DINOv2), and contrastive vision-language (CLIP) — arguing against a single-model or single-layer artifact, while scalar nuisance baselines (progress, time-to-go, task identity, proprioception) do not.
- **Ruling out task and temporal shortcuts.** A same-step matched-pair control groups rows by (task, timestep) and forms high/low pairs, paired with a label-shuffle negative control, showing the signal is not a proxy for task ID or elapsed time.
- **Behavioural usability.** The same offline-trained probe, plugged unchanged into a best-of-K test-time selection loop over π₀.₅'s own sampled action prefixes (no policy update, no separately trained reward model), measurably raises success on hard manipulation tasks — turning "what the representation contains" into "what it can change".
- **A staged, falsifiable protocol.** The three stages (decodability → matched control → online selection) are deliberately kept separate rather than collapsed into one headline number, and the paper records where the signal stops being useful (a no-gain task, negative cross-benchmark task splits, a real compute cost) rather than smoothing those away.

## Methodology
- **Value-like target.** For a successful language-conditioned manipulation trajectory of length T, timestep t receives a discounted Monte-Carlo target v_t = γ^(T−1−t) with γ = 0.99; failed trajectories get v_t = 0 throughout. γ = 0.99 yields an effective horizon of roughly 100 steps, matched to LIBERO-Goal episodes (capped at 300 steps, most successes completing under 150). Monte-Carlo returns from completed trajectories are used instead of bootstrapped value estimates, removing the need for a separate value network and the instabilities of bootstrapping on frozen features; the cost is a noisier target, the benefit is that probe success reflects genuine representation content rather than a secondary learning artifact. The authors explicitly call this *value-like*, not a Bellman-consistent value function.
- **Offline probing.** For each frozen feature family a standardized linear ridge probe v̂_t = wᵀφ̄_m(o_t, g) + b is fit, with the ridge penalty cross-validated over a fixed grid; R², Spearman correlation, RMSE/MAE, and per-task R² are reported on held-out examples. Two split regimes: a *demo split* (trajectories grouped by suite, task, and source demonstration; train/test groups sampled within each task) and a stricter *task split* (groups defined by suite and task, so held-out examples come from unseen tasks). Task identity, progress, and time-to-go are never privileged covariates for the VLA probes — they are trained as separate baseline rows.
- **Deconfounding.** Rows are grouped by task name and timestep; within each group, high/low pairs are formed whose value-like labels differ by at least 0.20. A probe is correct if the higher-label row receives the higher score; ties get half credit. A label-shuffle control randomly swaps which row counts as the positive member of each pair.
- **Test-time candidate selection.** With the frozen π₀.₅ policy, each replan step samples K = 16 action chunks using deterministic seeds derived from the episode seed, replan index, and candidate index; only a five-action prefix (h = 5) is executed. A simulator-backed teacher selector restores the simulator to the current snapshot for each candidate prefix, rolls out the prefix, and records (i) whether the short rollout reaches success, (ii) accumulated simulator reward r_k, and (iii) the probe score s_k on the frozen feature of the resulting observation. Selection rule: k* = argmax_{k∈S} s_k if the set S of succeeding prefixes is non-empty, otherwise argmax_k [z(r_k) + z(s_k)] with within-candidate-set normalization. The simulator is then restored and only the chosen prefix is committed. Baselines: *random* (same candidate set, uniform choice) and *greedy* (execute the decoded chunk directly).
- **Goal-conditioning diagnostics.** Goal-swap controls substitute the language instruction while holding the visual observation fixed and ask whether the probe score moves; a vision-only run (goal embedding bypassed) serves as a must-be-exactly-zero negative control.

## Experiments & Key Numbers
- **Scale.** LIBERO-Goal is the main benchmark; offline probing uses 311,719 frame-level rows from 1,400 mixed successful and failed trajectories. CALVIN-D (82,436 rows) and RobotWin provide cross-benchmark checks.
- **Table 1 (offline R², demo / task split).** π₀.₅ vision encoder 0.7377 / **0.5510** (best task split); OpenVLA-OFT LLM layer 07 **0.7561** (best demo) / 0.5505; OpenVLA vision backbone 0.7553 / 0.5493; OpenVLA-v0.1 0.7354 / 0.5429; SmolVLA 0.7028 / 0.5257; DINOv2 0.6899 / 0.5104; CLIP 0.6747 / 0.5095. Nuisance baselines: random projection 0.5568 / 0.3916; proprioception 0.2010 / 0.1107; progress and time-to-go both 0.0325 / 0.0302; task one-hot −0.0009 / −0.0018.
- **The informative exception.** π₀ — a flow-matching action head built on a VLM — reaches only R² = 0.0702 on the task split, close to the scalar baselines, whereas π₀.₅ in the same lineage reaches 0.5510. The authors read this as the value-like signal living in the parts of the stack that still encode goal-conditioned scene state, and being squeezed out where the representation has been compressed toward action-token prediction.
- **Table 2 (same-step matched control).** Primary probe: 4,605 pairs, pairwise accuracy **94.22%** vs. 50.05% label-shuffle, gap +44.17 pp. Seed/layer robustness (10 × 4,605 pairs): 92.16% vs. 49.67%, +42.49 pp. Per task: open middle drawer 1,053 pairs, 92.31% / 51.19% (+41.12); push plate 1,892 pairs, 94.56% / 50.79% (+43.77); wine rack 1,660 pairs, 95.06% / 48.49% (+46.57). Across the ten same-step runs (two feature choices, `vlm_final_output` and `vlm_layer_08`, five probe seeds each), pairwise accuracy ranges 89.58%–94.22% and shuffle stays within 49.01%–50.05%; no probe configuration falls below 89.58% and no shuffle configuration rises above 51.19%.

Push plate — 80/300 (26.7%), 101/300 (33.7%), **133/300 (44.3%)**; +10.67, +17.67; p = 0.00294; 2.10×. Wine rack — 107/300 (35.7%), 108/300 (36.0%), **132/300 (44.0%)**; +8.00, +8.33; p = 0.0264; 2.01×.
- **Boundary (negative result).** Open middle drawer moves only +2.00 against random and +0.67 against greedy — within sampling noise — and is reported as a no-gain case.
- **Task regimes (Table 4).** *Hard non-ceiling* is defined as greedy success ≤ 40%. Qualifying: open middle drawer 38.67% (150 episodes), wine rack 35.67% (300), push plate 26.67% (300). Open top drawer 82.00% (50) is an early screening task; bowl on stove 98.00% and bowl on cabinet 100.00% (50 each) are ceiling cases excluded by construction.
- **Compute cost.** Value-guided selection takes roughly 2× random selection per episode and more than 20× greedy decoding. In wall-time-per-extra-success terms: push plate ≈ 39 minutes of additional compute per additional success over random, wine rack ≈ 47 minutes, drawer (essentially no extra successes) ≈ 185 minutes. Probes themselves train in under five minutes on a single GPU; the dominant cost is simulator-backed candidate evaluation.
- **Goal-swap diagnostics.** The vision-only negative control produces exactly zero feature change and exactly zero score change across 14,080 pairs. π₀.₅ VLM features do move under language swaps: mean feature L₂ shift 1.96 with cosine similarity 0.9985; the score moves by a mean delta of +0.12 on the seed-7 `vlm_final_output` probe, with the original goal scoring higher on 53.7% of pairs. A second seed and a deeper VLM layer give means of −0.08 and −0.02, so the sign is not uniformly aligned with the original goal (drawer-style tasks tend to score lower on the original goal; rack and container tasks higher). The probe therefore inherits a goal-conditioned representation but does not behave as a calibrated reward model.
- **Cross-benchmark probing (Table 5).** CALVIN-D demo split: OpenVLA L20, R² = 0.3007 (ρ = 0.6034); task split: OpenVLA L07, R² = 0.0393 (ρ = 0.3434). RobotWin demo split (5 seeds) fits strongly — π₀.₅ vision 0.8715 (seed range 0.8452–0.8916), π₀.₅ VLM final 0.8407, π₀.₅ layer 08 0.8276 — but the task split has negative R² (−0.8528, −0.9314, −0.7790) with only weakly positive Spearman correlations (ρ = 0.1088, 0.1848, 0.1330). These rows are used as evidence for within-benchmark decodability beyond LIBERO, not for robust cross-task transfer.
- **Supporting runs (Table 6; not pooled with the balanced production estimates).** π₀.₅ full, n = 50 each: open middle drawer 32% → 48% (+16 pp), push plate 32% → 46% (+14), wine rack 40% → 48% (+8). OpenVLA 50ep: open middle drawer 38% → 70% (+32), open top drawer 40% → 52% (+12). K sweep: push plate K = 8 +10 pp, wine rack K = 8 +4 pp, push plate K = 32 +14 pp, wine rack K = 32 −2 pp (mixed). Temperature sweep (n = 20): open middle t = 0.7, 25% → 90% (+65 pp); open middle t = 0.3, 65% → 55% (−10, mixed); open top t = 0.3, 15% → 30% (+15); open top t = 0.7, 55% → 55% (0, mixed).
- **Why K = 16.** An early two-task sweep showed overall success climbing from 60% at K = 4 and K = 8 to 80% at K = 16; the candidate set needed enough breadth to contain a meaningfully better prefix. This is treated as design calibration, not formal validation.
- **Experimental controls.** Episodes capped at 300 environment steps; each ridge probe trained over five random seeds on a fixed regularization grid; online rollouts distributed across three independent servers with seed assignment fixed in advance per server (1111–3333 for hard-3, 4444–6666 for the push-plate follow-up, 7777–9999 for the wine-rack follow-up). Absolute success rates use Wilson 95% confidence intervals; strategy contrasts are computed on paired units sharing run family, server, task, and episode index, with paired bootstrap 95% CIs and exact McNemar p-values, and Fisher exact tests as an unpaired sensitivity check.

## Limitations
- No claim of a Bellman-consistent value function inside VLA models; the probe target is outcome-derived.
- Online gains are task-dependent, appearing only where the base policy is weak enough to leave headroom (greedy ≤ 40%) and the sampled candidate set actually contains alternatives differing in downstream outcome.
- The selector depends on simulator-backed candidate evaluation (≈ 2× random, more than an order of magnitude slower than greedy), so it should be read as a diagnostic intervention rather than a deployment-ready controller; making it practical would require cheaper candidate evaluation or a learned scorer that can replace simulator access.
