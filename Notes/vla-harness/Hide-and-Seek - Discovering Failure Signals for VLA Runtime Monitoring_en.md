# Hide-and-Seek in Trajectories: Discovering Failure Signals for VLA Runtime Monitoring

> arXiv: 2605.30834 | Year: 2026

## Topic
Coarsely supervised VLA failure detection

## Background
Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models let robots follow natural language instructions and generalize across diverse tasks, yet they remain vulnerable to execution failures in real-world deployment: a subtle grasp failure, if undetected, can cascade into a dropped object or a costly recovery. Detecting such failures *during* execution is therefore central to robust deployment of embodied systems. This work, from the University of Wisconsin–Madison and the Georgia Institute of Technology, casts runtime failure detection as a coarsely supervised learning problem in which only trajectory-level outcome labels are available.

## Limitations & Research Problem
- **Limitation:** Existing failure detectors fall short along two axes. (1) *Supervision cost*: step-level failure annotation is expensive and hard to scale, requiring experts to mark precise error timestamps in long-horizon, stochastic trajectories. Recent work (SAFE) sidesteps this by uniformly assigning the trajectory-level label to every timestep, which mislabels normal actions before failure onset and injects substantial label noise. (2) *Computational practicality*: action-resampling methods and external VLM judges incur inference overhead that precludes real-time deployment; OOD-based detectors generalize poorly to unseen tasks; and most prior methods target specialist policies on fixed task sets rather than open-world generalist VLAs.
- **Problem:** Given only trajectory-level success/failure labels and **no step-level annotation**, how can one learn a lightweight, real-time fine-grained failure detector that localizes failure-indicative actions and raises alarms in a timely manner (Definition 3.1, Coarsely Supervised Failure Detection)?

## Contributions
- **Hide-and-Seek**, a lightweight runtime failure detection framework that draws a novel connection between coarsely supervised learning and embodied failure detection, discovering failure-indicative actions from trajectory-level supervision alone.
- A two-granularity contrastive objective that distinguishes failure-indicative from non-failure actions both *across* trajectories and *within* a trajectory, converting coarse trajectory-level supervision into a **temporally structured** failure signal.
- Comprehensive evaluation on LIBERO and VLABench in simulation and on a real robot, across three representative VLA policies spanning autoregressive and flow-matching paradigms (OpenVLA, π0, π0.5), achieving state-of-the-art performance on both seen and unseen tasks with a practical accuracy–timeliness trade-off.

## Methodology
- **Formulation:** At each timestep, a per-step action embedding $h_t \in \mathbb{R}^d$ is extracted from the VLA's action token or action head; a rollout is $\tau = (h_1,\dots,h_T)$ with only a trajectory-level label $y(\tau)\in\{0,1\}$. A sequential detector $f_\phi$ with sigmoid output maps each prefix $\tau_{\le t}$ to a failure score $s_t \in [0,1]$.
- **Inter-trajectory contrastive loss (Eq. 2):** A margin-based loss requiring the *most* failure-indicative step in a failure trajectory to score above the hardest false-positive step in a successful trajectory (e.g., a momentary hesitation or an awkward but recovered intermediate pose), with margin $m_r$. It makes no assumption about *where* the failure occurs, adaptively "seeking" the hidden failure signal.
- **Intra-trajectory contrastive loss (Eq. 3):** Defines a proxy failure onset $t_{\text{onset}} = \arg\max_t (s_t - s_{t-1})$ (the sharpest score increase) and enforces the average post-onset score to exceed the average pre-onset score by margin $m_o$, shaping score dynamics without any temporal annotation. Section C.1 shows this proxy closely approximates the annotated onset.
- **Overall objective (Eq. 4):** $\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{inter}} + \lambda \mathcal{L}_{\text{intra}}$.
- **Implementation:** Default backbone is a single-layer LSTM. For autoregressive policies (OpenVLA) embeddings are averaged across layers and degrees of freedom; for flow-matching policies (π0, π0.5) hidden states are taken before the velocity prediction head at the final denoising step and aligned across action chunks. Non-overlapping sliding-window average pooling with window size $w$ mitigates functional temporal redundancy.
- **Runtime monitoring via conformal prediction (Eq. 5):** A one-sided functional CP procedure calibrates a time-varying threshold $\zeta_t = \mu_t + b_t$ on $C$ held-out successful trajectories; under exchangeability and a user-specified significance level $\alpha$, the trajectory-level false alarm rate on new successful rollouts is bounded by $\alpha$. Failure is declared at the earliest $t$ with $s_t \ge \zeta_t$ (the detection time).
- **Metrics and baselines:** Balanced accuracy (bACC, primary), weighted accuracy (wACC), and time-weighted accuracy (TWA, penalizing late detection); results averaged over $\alpha \in \{0.15, 0.20, 0.25\}$ and 3 random seeds. Twelve action-based baselines span OOD-based (Mahalanobis, Cosine k-NN, PCA-KMeans, RND, LogpZO), multi-sampling ($N=10$ per step; Cluster Entropy, EigenScore, STAC, ACE), classifier-based (SAFE-LSTM, SAFE-MLP), and token-uncertainty methods (Entropy, NLL, OpenVLA only).

## Experiments & Key Numbers
- **LIBERO-10 (Table 1):** With OpenVLA (success rate 51.0%), Hide-and-Seek reaches seen bACC 0.852 / wACC 0.853 / TWA 0.660 and unseen 0.834 / 0.828 / 0.663, best across all baselines. With π0 (success rate 84.2%): seen 0.885 / 0.926 / 0.693, unseen 0.892 / 0.921 / 0.705.
- **OOD generalization gap:** Mahalanobis drops from 67.0% bACC on seen tasks to 51.3% on unseen tasks with OpenVLA.
- **VLABench (Table 2):** With π0.5 (success rate 40.3%), seen bACC 0.856 / wACC 0.827 / TWA 0.662 and unseen 0.713 / 0.709 / 0.608 — outperforming SAFE-MLP by +6.8% bACC and +8.1% TWA on seen tasks, and +7.2% bACC and +10.8% TWA on unseen tasks.
- **Real robot (Table 3; UFactory xArm 6 with Intel RealSense D435, π0.5):** CUBE success rate 61.2%, KITCHEN 63.7%. Hide-and-Seek achieves the best TWA in all four settings: CUBE seen 0.966 bACC / 0.852 TWA, unseen 0.914 / 0.800; KITCHEN seen 0.968 / 0.863, unseen 0.972 / 0.876. On unseen CUBE it gains +11.7% bACC and +15.0% TWA over SAFE-MLP, whose performance swings from 99.2% bACC on seen CUBE to 79.7% on unseen. Multi-sampling baselines are excluded from real-robot experiments due to inference overhead.
- **Versus a VLM-based runtime monitor (Table 6):** Prompting Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct for per-frame success/failure judgments on LIBERO-10 with OpenVLA yields bACC 0.712 at 2.343 s per step; Hide-and-Seek reaches bACC 0.843 at 0.001 s per step on a single NVIDIA A6000 — **+13.1% bACC at over 2,000× higher speed**.
- **Versus uniform trajectory-level labeling (Fig. 4a):** +19.4% bACC on OpenVLA and +18.6% bACC on π0.
- **Loss component ablation (Table 5):** $\mathcal{L}_{\text{inter}}$ alone already reaches 0.825 (OpenVLA) / 0.856 (π0), surpassing the strongest baseline SAFE-MLP by +2.6% / +2.1%; $\mathcal{L}_{\text{intra}}$ alone degrades to 0.579 / 0.601; combining both gives 0.843 / 0.888 (+1.8% / +3.2% over inter alone).
- **Backbone ablation (Table 4):** LSTM 0.843 / 0.888, GRU 0.834 / 0.893, Transformer 0.804 / 0.824, MLP 0.765 / 0.743 — context-utilizing backbones clearly beat the per-step MLP.
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity (Figs. 4b, 4c):** Across the margin sweep, bACC varies by less than 4% from the mean; the best setting is $m_r=1.0, m_o=0.5$ (88.8% bACC). Optimal window size is $w=4$ for OpenVLA and $w=8$ for π0, the latter aligning with π0's action-chunk replanning interval.
- **Accuracy–timeliness trade-off (Fig. 3):** Sweeping $\alpha \in [0.01, 1.0]$ traces operating curves; Hide-and-Seek lies closest to the top-left for both OpenVLA and π0. Reference failure onsets were annotated with GPT-5.2 on recorded videos.
