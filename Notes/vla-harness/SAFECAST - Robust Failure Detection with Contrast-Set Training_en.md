# SAFECAST: Robust Failure Detection for VLA Policies with Contrast-Set Training and Calibration

> arXiv: 2608.04246 | Year: 2026

## Topic

Contrast-set calibrated VLA failure detection

## Background

Vision-language-action (VLA) policies such as OpenVLA and π₀ generalize well on clean, training-like distributions but degrade substantially under deployment-time shifts: clutter, distractor objects, lighting variation, novel objects, altered robot initial states, and reworded instructions. These failures matter in safety-critical settings such as assistive robotics, human-robot interaction, and manipulation of fragile objects. The dominant runtime-failure-detection line of work is exemplified by SAFE (Gu et al., NeurIPS 2025): freeze the VLA policy, train a lightweight MLP probe on its pre-final-layer hidden state $h_t$ to emit a per-timestep failure score $r_t = f_\phi(h_t)$, and use functional conformal prediction (FCP) to turn $r_t$ into a time-varying intervention threshold $\delta_t$ that flags unsafe rollouts before task failure occurs. This paper is from USC (Rajaprakash, Prajapati, Xue, Anwar, Thomason), funded in part by a DARPA ARC SAFRON grant.

## Limitations & Research Problem

- **Limitation:** The reliability of SAFE-style detectors rests on one key assumption — that calibration trajectories resemble deployment trajectories (the exchangeability premise of conformal prediction). Both probe training and conformal calibration draw only from the source distribution $\mathcal{D}_\mathrm{src}$. Under visual, language, or combined multimodal shift, the distribution of hidden-state risk trajectories changes substantially, so thresholds $\delta_t$ calibrated on source-distribution rollouts become misaligned, producing delayed or missed failure warnings.
- **Problem:** For a frozen VLA policy, how can rollout-level failure detection remain reliable when the deployment distribution $\mathcal{D}_\mathrm{eval}$ differs from $\mathcal{D}_\mathrm{src}$? Concretely: can cheap contrast-set perturbations reshape both the probe's training distribution and the conformal calibration distribution so that risk trajectories better approximate deployment conditions?

## Contributions

- Introduces SAFECAST (**Sc**alable **F**ailure **E**stimation with **C**ontrast-**S**et **A**ugmentation for **S**afety **T**racking): visual, language, and joint visual-language contrast-set rollouts are injected into **both** probe training and functional conformal calibration. The intervention is at the level of data distributions, not model architecture.
- Statistically significant ROC-AUC gains over the SAFE baseline on real-world DROID (Franka, with π₀ and π₀-FAST) and LIBERO simulation (OpenVLA and π₀); exact $p$ values are in the paper's Appendix C.
- Disentangles the roles of augmented training vs. augmented calibration via three ablations (TrainAug / CalAug / full SAFECAST) and shows their relative benefit is policy-dependent.
- Shows joint visual-language contrast sets outperform single-modality perturbations.
- Demonstrates a sim-to-real calibration recipe: train the probe entirely in simulation, then perform functional conformal calibration on a smaller set of real-world contrast-set trajectories — outperforming more expensive real-world probe training.
- Revises SAFE's evaluation protocol: SAFE splits LIBERO-Spatial into seen/unseen tasks drawn from the same distribution, whereas SAFECAST explicitly separates source, calibration, and deployment distributions to measure robustness under controlled deployment shift.

## Methodology

- **Setup:** The policy $\pi$ is frozen throughout — it is never updated during probe training, conformal calibration, or evaluation. The probe is a lightweight MLP producing $r_t = f_\phi(h_t)$; over a rollout of length $T$ it yields a temporally aligned risk sequence $r_{1:T}$.
- **Calibration (the focus — genuinely conformal):** SAFECAST uses **functional conformal prediction** (following the functional prediction-band formulation of Diquigiovanni et al., *Statistica Sinica* 2024), not plain split conformal. On calibration rollouts $\tau^\mathrm{cal}_j$, the trained probe produces risk trajectories $r^{(j)}_{1:T}$; FCP computes nonconformity scores $S_\mathrm{cal}$ over the **successful** calibration trajectories to construct a one-sided time-varying threshold $\delta_t = \mu_t + q_\alpha$. At deployment, a rollout is flagged as headed for failure whenever $r_t > \delta_t$. The significance level $\alpha \in (0,1)$ sets the operational risk tolerance: lower $\alpha$ gives a higher threshold (fewer false positives, more conservative intervention), higher $\alpha$ a lower threshold and higher FPR. **SAFECAST's change is to replace the calibration pool $\mathcal{D}^\mathrm{cal}_{\phi,\mathrm{src}}$ with the contrast-set-augmented $\mathcal{D}^\mathrm{cal}_{\phi,\mathrm{aug}}$** drawn from $\mathcal{D}_\mathrm{aug} = \mathcal{D}_\mathrm{src} \cup \mathcal{D}_\mathrm{CS}$, and likewise the training pool with $\mathcal{D}^\mathrm{train}_{\phi,\mathrm{aug}}$. Calibration subsets are sampled with controlled success and failure counts where possible, so threshold estimation is not dominated by a single outcome class.
- **Contrast-set construction (key detail):** Controlled perturbation operators are applied to the visual and/or language inputs of source rollouts, and the frozen policy is **re-executed** under the perturbed condition, yielding $\tau^\mathrm{CS} = \{(\tilde o_t, \tilde l, \tilde a_t)\}_{t=1}^T$. Because the policy is re-executed rather than relabeled, $\tau^\mathrm{CS}$ is **not** a counterfactual or relabeled copy of $\tau^\mathrm{src}$ — both the action sequence $\tilde a_{1:T}$ and the hidden-state trajectory may differ. The authors therefore explicitly **do not assume trajectory-level exchangeability** between $\mathcal{D}_\mathrm{src}$ and $\mathcal{D}_\mathrm{CS}$; contrast sets are instead used to construct training and calibration distributions whose risk trajectories better approximate the shifts expected in $\mathcal{D}_\mathrm{eval}$.
  - **Visual contrast sets** perturb the observation stream with the instruction held fixed. In LIBERO-Spatial, distractor objects are added subject to three constraints: they do not change the intended task, are not referenced in the instruction, and do not obstruct the nominal source-distribution rollout. In real-world DROID they include distractor objects, cluttered backgrounds, novel objects, and altered object configurations.
  - **Language contrast sets** perturb the instruction with the visual scene held fixed. In LIBERO-Spatial, three paraphrases per task are generated by prompting the ChatGPT 5.1 Instant model, designed to preserve the intended target object, action, and goal location; to reduce rollout-collection cost, each paraphrase is evaluated on three episodes per task rather than all 50 source episodes. In DROID they include paraphrased instructions and negated or distractor phrasing where applicable.
  - **Joint visual-language contrast sets** perturb both streams simultaneously, inducing multimodal deployment shifts. These are intended to approximate exactly the conditions that may violate the exchangeability assumptions underlying source-distribution conformal calibration; examples are primarily in the real setting.
- **Candidate filtering:** Candidate training rollouts are filtered by an active rejection procedure based on Dynamic Time Warping (DTW). Candidates whose trajectories fall below a fixed DTW distance threshold from already-selected rollouts are rejected, reducing near-duplicates in $\mathcal{D}^\mathrm{train}_{\phi,\mathrm{CS}}$.
- **Four configurations:** SAFE (source training + source calibration), SAFECAST_TrainAug (augmented training only), SAFECAST_CalAug (augmented calibration only), and full SAFECAST (both).
- **Metrics:** F1 and ROC-AUC are swept across the conformal significance level $\alpha$. The paper reports $\alpha$-marginalized F1 (averaged over the sweep, capturing robustness across risk tolerances rather than performance at one selected threshold), and computes TPR/FPR at each $\alpha$, reporting ROC-AUC as the area under the resulting TPR–FPR curve.

## Experiments & Key Numbers

- **Setup:** Real-world experiments use a Franka/DROID setup with π₀ and π₀-FAST. For π₀, the open-source DROID checkpoint is LoRA-finetuned on 20 teleoperated spatial pick-and-place demonstrations, because the zero-shot checkpoint achieved zero successful rollouts in preliminary testing; π₀-FAST is evaluated without additional finetuning since it already achieved nonzero task success. Evaluation rollouts $\tau^\mathrm{eval}$ contain stronger shifts including novel objects, new tasks, clutter, lighting variation, and altered robot initial states. Simulation uses LIBERO-Spatial and LIBERO-Plus with OpenVLA and π₀. All numbers are averaged over $\alpha \in \{0.1, 0.2, \dots, 0.9\}$ and **30 seeds**.
- **Table 1 (SAFE → SAFECAST):**

  | Method | F1 Sim-π₀ | F1 Sim-OpenVLA | F1 Real-π₀ | F1 Real-π₀-FAST | AUC Sim-π₀ | AUC Sim-OpenVLA | AUC Real-π₀ | AUC Real-π₀-FAST |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | Always guess failure | 0.5266 | 0.8314 | **0.7322** | 0.7654 | 0.0000 | 0.0000 | 0.0000 | 0.0000 |
  | SAFE | 0.7132 | 0.8589 | 0.4085 | 0.7475 | 0.3267 | 0.5937 | 0.2626 | 0.5501 |
  | SAFECAST_TrainAug | 0.7516 | 0.8687 | 0.5838 | **0.8109** | 0.4278\* | 0.6514 | 0.2649 | 0.6621\* |
  | SAFECAST_CalAug | 0.7373 | 0.8628 | 0.4480 | 0.7534 | 0.4382\* | 0.6782\* | 0.3415\* | 0.6578\* |
  | **SAFECAST** | **0.7528** | **0.8728** | 0.5412 | 0.8053 | **0.4469\*** | **0.8014** | **0.3807\*** | **0.6664\*** |

  (\* indicates statistical significance, tested for ROC-AUC across paired performance at each $\alpha$ threshold.) Absolute ROC-AUC gains of SAFECAST over SAFE: Sim-π₀ +0.1202, Sim-OpenVLA +0.2077, Real-π₀ +0.1181, Real-π₀-FAST +0.1163.
- **Policy-dependent augmentation benefit:** On real-world DROID, π₀ gains most from augmented **probe training** (F1 0.4085 → 0.5838), suggesting that exposure to perturbed rollouts during representation learning substantially improves downstream failure separability. π₀-FAST benefits more strongly from SAFECAST **calibration**, which the authors read as evidence that calibration mismatch is the larger bottleneck for stronger pretrained policies. In simulation the same pattern holds: OpenVLA benefits most from augmented probe training, while π₀ is most robust under full SAFECAST training plus calibration.
- **Modality ablation (Figure 4, simulation):** Mean F1 by contrast-set modality — visual only: OpenVLA 0.871 / π₀ 0.649; language only: 0.872 / 0.664; **visual + language: 0.873 / 0.753** (best for both policies, with the joint gain most pronounced for π₀). The text additionally reports that on OpenVLA, multimodal contrast sets improve F1 from **0.922** under visual-only augmentation to **0.959**. The takeaway: deployment-time calibration benefits from covering the joint perturbation space encountered during execution, rather than modeling visual or language shifts independently.
- **Sim-to-real (Figure 5):** A probe trained entirely on LIBERO-Spatial trajectories, with functional conformal calibration performed on real-world DROID contrast-set trajectories, outperforms the DROID π₀ SAFECAST variant trained and calibrated only on the smaller real-world dataset — across the full $\alpha$ range. SAFECAST thus enables sim-to-real transfer even under deployment mismatch, at lower data-collection cost.
- **Negative / boundary results worth noting:** On Real-π₀, the trivial "Always guess failure" baseline attains the highest F1 (0.7322), above every learned detector (SAFECAST 0.5412, TrainAug 0.5838) — in that setting the failure rate is high enough that F1 favors the majority class, and ROC-AUC is the more trustworthy separability metric (SAFECAST 0.3807 vs. 0.0000 for the trivial baseline). Absolute real-world ROC-AUC also remains modest (0.3807 for π₀, 0.6664 for π₀-FAST).
- **Limitations (authors' own):** SAFECAST depends on collecting contrast-set trajectories representative of likely deployment shifts; if the robot encounters perturbations outside the contrast-set distribution, performance may still degrade. Contrast-set collection introduces extra rollout cost, and the current perturbation families cover only a subset of possible visual, language, and task-level changes. Critically, the method **improves calibration alignment empirically but does not restore formal conformal exchangeability guarantees under arbitrary deployment shift**.
- **Relation to SAFE, summarized:** SAFECAST leaves SAFE's probe architecture and FCP machinery unchanged and alters only *which data trains the probe and which data calibrates the conformal threshold*, while also replacing SAFE's same-distribution seen/unseen LIBERO-Spatial split with an explicit source / calibration / deployment separation for controlled shift measurement.
