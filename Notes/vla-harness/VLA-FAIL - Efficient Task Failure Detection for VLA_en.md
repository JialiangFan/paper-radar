# VLA-FAIL: Efficient Task Failure Detection for Finetuned Vision-Language-Action Models

> arXiv: 2606.21386 | Year: 2026

## Topic
Runtime failure detection for VLAs

## Background
Vision-language-action models (VLAs) achieve state-of-the-art performance on robotic manipulation, yet still behave unpredictably in out-of-distribution scenarios even after finetuning on the downstream task. Runtime failure detection is therefore a prerequisite for safe real-world deployment, enabling human intervention or safe fallbacks as early as possible. The work (KIT Autonomous Learning Robots Lab) validates its detectors on two SOTA VLAs, π0.5 and X-VLA.

## Limitations & Research Problem
- **Limitation:** Existing VLA failure detectors are constrained in three ways: (1) they require computationally expensive action sampling (ACE and Diff use 32 action samples), making them non-real-time; (2) they rely on architectural assumptions or external models (VLM reasoning, object-centric perception, video models), limiting applicability; (3) supervised formulations such as SAFE need failure rollouts, which are costly and potentially unsafe to collect. Purely novelty-based detection is also insufficient: OOD states do not necessarily imply task failure, while some failures arise from internally inconsistent action generation without any obvious visual anomaly.
- **Problem:** Can a general runtime detector for VLAs require no failure data, no external model, and negligible compute, while still detecting failures accurately and early? And how can accuracy and detection latency be evaluated jointly in a threshold-independent way?

## Contributions
- **VLA-FAIL**, a lightweight runtime failure detection framework combining two complementary detectors, requiring no failure data and no auxiliary model, with minimal computational overhead.
- **LLMD** (last-layer token-wise Mahalanobis distance), extending Mahalanobis-based OOD detection from classifiers to flow-matching VLAs by removing sampling-induced feature variation via a fixed prior noise sample.
- **ACC** (action chunk consistency), a velocity-normalized inconsistency score over the overlap between consecutive action chunks induced by receding-horizon control, computed from already generated actions.
- **AUCPDT** (area under the penalized detection time curve), a threshold-independent metric jointly capturing precision, recall, and detection time.
- Extensive evaluation on six real-world tasks and the Libero-Plus simulation benchmark, showing LLMD and ACC capture complementary failure modes.

## Methodology
- **Setup:** At timestep t the VLA maps an observation o to a chunk of H actions $a_{1:H}$; under receding-horizon control only the first R < H actions are executed before re-querying. The goal is to predict failure before the episode horizon T.
- **Fixed prior noise:** In flow matching, last-layer features depend on o, the flow timestep t, and the noisy action $a_t$, injecting observation-independent noise. The paper fixes a single sample $a_0^* \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$ and evaluates $f^*(o) := f(o, t=0, a_0^*)$ at t = 0, where $p_0(a \mid o) = \mathcal{N}(a; 0, I)$, so no artificial covariate shift is introduced. This needs one forward pass that runs in parallel with action sampling.
- **LLMD:** After finetuning, one gradient-free pass over the dataset $\mathcal{D}$ yields token-wise means $\mu_s$ and covariances $\Sigma_s$ (regularized as $\Sigma_s + \lambda I$, $\lambda \ll 1$). During rollout the score is the **maximum** squared Mahalanobis distance across token positions $s \in \{1,\dots,H\}$, so a single unlikely feature suffices to flag failure. No temporal smoothing is required.
- **ACC:** Compares the unexecuted suffix $a^{t-1}_{R+1:H}$ of the previous chunk with the prefix $a^t_{1:H-R}$ of the next one via mean absolute error per dimension, normalized by the average velocity $v_d^t$ (clamped at $v_{\min}$ to avoid inflation for a near-stationary robot). In practice only the D = 3 predicted absolute end-effector position is used. Since occasional replanning is normal, an exponential moving average with $\alpha = 0.9$ is applied; strong smoothing is required for good detection rates.
- **Fusion:** $F_{\text{FAIL}}(t) := (s^t_{\text{ACC}} \ge \tau_{\text{ACC}}) \lor (s^t_{\text{LLMD}} \ge \tau_{\text{LLMD}})$, with thresholds set by time-constant conformal prediction on calibration data. Time-dependent thresholds are rejected because they do not apply to episodes of highly variable length (e.g. the real-world Drawer task). For evaluation, both scores are rank-transformed to a uniform scale and the minimum is taken, a task-agnostic surrogate for the logical OR.
- **AUCPDT:** For a failed episode, PDT is the normalized first timestep with score ≥ τ, or 1 if the failure is missed (i.e. the baseline of automatic detection at the horizon). Precision and expected PDT are computed at every unique threshold, Pareto-optimal points are retained, and the curve is integrated over the precision interval with right-hand Riemann sums; lower is better.

## Experiments & Key Numbers
- **Models and baselines:** π0.5 (3.6B, PaliGemma VLM, action-expert/VLM fusion via cross-attention at every layer) and X-VLA (0.9B, Florence-2-Large encoder-decoder VLM, conditioning only on final encoder features). Baselines ACE, STAC and Diff all use 32 action samples and are evaluated on replays of policy rollouts since they are not real-time capable (RTX 5090, a constant 17 ms of VLM excluded).
- **Scale:** Six real-world tasks (Blocks, Stack T, Cups, Kitchen, Drawer, Mixer) covering high precision, multi-modal demonstrations, long multi-stage episodes and language conditioning; roughly 80 rollouts per policy with 3 seeds per rollout. Libero-Plus (Object / Spatial / Goal / 10 suites) is evaluated with a single seed.
- **Overall:** FAIL is the only method placing in the top three on nearly all real-world and simulated tasks, with only marginal overhead. On Kitchen with X-VLA, ACC, LLMD and FAIL all reach PR 1.00 (FAIL PDT 0.21). On Stack T with X-VLA, FAIL reaches PR 0.98 / PDT 0.08 versus ACE 0.96 / 0.09 and Diff 0.97 / 0.08.
- **Complementarity:** ACC detects failures more reliably while LLMD detects them earlier, so FAIL attains the lowest overall AUCPDT on Cups, Stack T and Mixer (particularly for π0.5) and for X-VLA on the Libero-Plus suites. ACC works well when the robot exhibits rapid, jerky motion; LLMD is stronger when the policy repeatedly retries an action in an infinite loop or falls back to "default" actions unrelated to the environment state.
- **Token-wise ablation (Table 3):** Global feature statistics degrade performance markedly — AUCPDT worsens from 0.19 to 0.24 for X-VLA on Drawer, and on Kitchen PR drops from 1.00 to 0.67 with PDT rising from 0.20 to 0.42.
- **Velocity normalization (Table 5):** Clear gains in simulation — for X-VLA on Spatial, AUCPDT drops from 0.38 to 0.28 and PR rises from 0.86 to 0.95. On real-world tasks the improvement is smaller but consistent (Table 4).
- **Chunk overlap (Figure 5):** ACC degrades as the overlap shrinks but retains some detection capability even with a single overlapping action; π0.5 is more robust to short overlaps than X-VLA.
- **ACC vs STAC:** ACC can be viewed as a velocity-normalized single-sample estimator of STAC and outperforms it on nearly all real-world tasks. On Libero-Plus STAC attains higher AUCPR (up to 1.00 on several suites) but ACC detects significantly earlier, hypothesized to be because ACC evaluates planning inconsistency only against executed chunks rather than counterfactual trajectories, reducing false detections during mode selection.
- **Limitations:** LLMD needs access to the finetuning data for one preprocessing pass, which may be expensive or infeasible; ACC requires receding-horizon control with sufficient action overlap and does not apply to fully open-loop chunk execution; VLA-FAIL can miss failures that are consistent in both features and actions (e.g. confidently stopping or ignoring a language instruction), where VLM-based semantic detection would be complementary.
