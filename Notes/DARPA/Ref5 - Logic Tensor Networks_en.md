# Logic Tensor Networks

## Research Problem
How to design a unified differentiable framework that grounds first-order logic into tensor computation graphs for end-to-end neurosymbolic learning.

## Topic
Neurosymbolic AI via Differentiable Logic

## Background
Combining logic and neural networks into neurosymbolic approaches has gained significant momentum in recent years. Modern deep learning, relying on purely sub-symbolic distributed representations, has achieved remarkable results in computer vision, natural language processing, and multimodal learning, yet remains data-inefficient and struggles with out-of-distribution generalization. Symbolic AI, conversely, excels at theorem proving, logical inference, and verification but falters when confronted with incomplete knowledge, noisy data, and high computational complexity. Neurosymbolic AI seeks to unite the strengths of both paradigms — combining rich knowledge representations (knowledge bases, ontologies, semantic networks) with data-driven learning from complex inputs (images, time series, natural language). A central challenge in this integration is the symbol grounding problem: how to map symbolic elements (constants, functions, predicates) onto real-valued data representations.

## Limitations & Research Problem
- **Limitation 1:** Purely sub-symbolic models lack comprehensibility and cannot explicitly represent or leverage rich relational knowledge expressed in first-order logic, leading to data inefficiency and poor generalization under distribution shift.
- **Limitation 2:** Purely symbolic AI systems suffer from high computational complexity, brittleness to incomplete or noisy data, and an inability to learn latent structures from high-dimensional raw inputs.
- **Limitation 3:** Existing neurosymbolic approaches lack a unified, fully differentiable logical language capable of simultaneously supporting classification, regression, clustering, relational learning, semi-supervised learning, and query answering within a single framework.
- **Problem:** How to design a general neurosymbolic framework in which the syntax and semantics of first-order logic are fully embedded into differentiable computational graphs, thereby preserving logical expressiveness while enabling end-to-end gradient-based learning?

## Contributions
- Introduces **Logic Tensor Networks (LTN)**, a neurosymbolic framework grounded in **Real Logic** — a fully differentiable first-order logic language with truth-values in the continuous interval [0,1] (fuzzy semantics), where every element of the logic signature is grounded onto real-valued tensors via neural computational graphs.
- Proposes the **Stable Product Real Logic** configuration: employs the product t-norm for conjunction, its dual t-conorm for disjunction, and the Reichenbach implication, augmented with projection functions that map truth-values into open intervals to eliminate vanishing and exploding gradients at boundary values.
- Introduces the **generalized mean aggregator** as a differentiable approximation of universal and existential quantifiers, with a hyperparameter p controlling the strictness of smooth minimum/maximum behavior, balancing flexibility and differentiability.
- Extends LTN with **explicit domain declarations** (typed constants, variables, and predicates), **guarded quantifiers** (conditional quantification over masked subsets), and **diagonal quantification** (efficient aligned quantification over sample-label pairs).
- Formally defines the three core tasks in Real Logic: **learning** (maximizing satisfiability of the knowledge base), **reasoning** (fuzzy logical consequence and refutation-based reasoning), and **query answering** (truth, value, generalization truth, and generalization value queries).
- Demonstrates that LTN uniformly handles binary classification, multi-class classification, multi-label classification, regression, clustering, semi-supervised learning, relational learning, embedding learning, and logical reasoning — all implemented in TensorFlow 2 with an open-source library.

## Methodology
- **Real Logic — Syntax and Semantics:** Defines a typed first-order language L with constant symbols C, function symbols F, predicate symbols P, and variable symbols X, all constrained by domain-typing functions D. Semantically, the classical notion of interpretation is replaced by a **grounding** G that maps constants to tensors, variables to sequences of tensors, functions to tensor-to-tensor operations, and predicates to functions outputting values in [0,1] (typically implemented as neural networks).
- **Fuzzy Semantics for Connectives and Quantifiers:** Conjunction (∧), disjunction (∨), implication (→), and negation (¬) are approximated by a t-norm T, t-conorm S, fuzzy implication I, and fuzzy negation N, respectively. Quantifiers ∀ and ∃ are approximated by aggregation operators — the generalized mean w.r.t. error (for ∀) and the generalized mean (for ∃) — where higher values of the exponent p yield stricter approximations of min/max.
- **Stable Product Configuration:** To resolve vanishing gradients that arise when the product t-norm encounters boundary truth-values (0 or 1), projection functions π₀ and π₁ map [0,1] to open intervals ]0,1] and [0,1[, ensuring non-zero gradients throughout the computation.
- **Knowledge Base Representation:** A Real Logic theory T = (K, G(·|θ), Θ) comprises: symbol groundings (explicit or parametric), a set K of closed first-order formulas encoding factual propositions and generalized knowledge, and the hyperparameters governing fuzzy operator semantics. Knowledge is expressed through fixed groundings (known feature vectors, training data as constants), parametric groundings (learnable embeddings, neural network weights), factual propositions (labeled examples), and generalized propositions (universally or existentially quantified formulas expressing domain constraints and relational rules).
- **Learning:** Defined as the optimization problem of finding parameters θ* that maximize the satisfiability of the theory — the aggregated truth-values of all formulas in K — via gradient-based optimization, with optional L1/L2 regularization. Learning constants corresponds to learning embeddings; learning functions corresponds to learning generative or regression models; learning predicates corresponds to learning classifiers.
- **Reasoning:** Defines fuzzy logical consequence and proposes two approaches: (1) *Querying after learning* — evaluating formula truth-values under the optimal grounding; (2) *Proof by refutation* — searching for a counter-example grounding that satisfies the knowledge base while violating the target formula, using an elu-based differentiable penalty function to enable gradient-based search over the dual problem.
- **Query Answering:** Supports four query types: truth queries (scalar truth-value of a closed formula), value queries (tensor value of a grounded term), generalization truth queries (truth-values of a formula on unseen data), and generalization value queries (regression outputs on unseen data).
- **Implementation:** Built on TensorFlow 2, where each logical operator is directly grounded as a node in the TensorFlow computational graph, enabling automatic differentiation and end-to-end optimization using the Adam optimizer with a learning rate of 0.001. Results are reported as averages over 10 runs with 95% confidence intervals.
