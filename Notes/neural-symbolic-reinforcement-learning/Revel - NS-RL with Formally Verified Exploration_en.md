# Revel: NS-RL with Formally Verified Exploration

## Topic
Verified Safe RL Exploration

## Background
Safe exploration is a fundamental challenge in reinforcement learning: in safety-critical applications such as autonomous driving and robotics, even low-probability unsafe actions can cause catastrophic failures, necessitating worst-case safety guarantees rather than merely probabilistic ones. Existing safe RL methods (e.g., Constrained Policy Optimization) rely on stochastic safety definitions and cannot provide formal worst-case guarantees. Prior approaches based on formal verification and shielding can offer provable safety, but are limited to finite action spaces, employ static shields constructed before training, and tend to be overly conservative in continuous control settings. Moreover, repeatedly invoking formal verification of neural networks inside a learning loop is computationally infeasible.

## Limitations & Research Problem
- **Limitation 1:** Probabilistic safe RL methods (e.g., CPO) cannot guarantee worst-case safety and frequently violate safety constraints during training.
- **Limitation 2:** Existing verified exploration approaches rely on static, pre-constructed shields that are not updated during learning, leading to overly conservative and suboptimal behavior in continuous state/action spaces.
- **Limitation 3:** Direct formal verification of neural network policies within a learning loop is computationally infeasible -- verifying a single network can take over half an hour with state-of-the-art techniques.
- **Problem:** How can one design an RL framework that supports continuous state/action spaces and deep policy gradient methods while guaranteeing that every intermediate policy during training is provably safe under worst-case dynamics?

## Contributions
- Introduces REVEL (Reinforcement learning with verified exploration), the first RL framework to enable formally verified safe exploration in continuous state/action spaces while using deep policy representations and policy gradient methods.
- Designs a neurosymbolic policy representation: $h(s) = \text{if } (P^\#(s, f(s)) \subseteq \phi) \text{ then } f(s) \text{ else } g(s)$, where $f$ is a neural network (normal mode), $g$ is a piecewise linear symbolic shield, and $\phi$ is an inductive invariant serving as a safety monitor. The shield and monitor are expressed in an efficiently verifiable symbolic form, entirely avoiding direct neural network verification.
- Proposes a learning algorithm based on functional mirror descent with three iterative steps: (i) Lift -- promote the symbolic shield into the neurosymbolic space; (ii) Update -- perform approximate policy gradient updates in the neural policy space; (iii) Project -- project the updated policy back into the safe symbolic shield space via imitation learning. No step requires neural network verification.
- Provides theoretical analysis proving that, under reasonable assumptions, REVEL achieves a regret bound of $O(\sigma\sqrt{1/T} + \epsilon + \beta + L_J\zeta)$, where lower shield invocation frequency $\zeta$ yields lower regret.
- Empirically demonstrates on 10 continuous control benchmarks that REVEL achieves zero safety violations in 8 out of 10 tasks (while DDPG and CPO exhibit numerous violations), matches or exceeds DDPG performance in 7 out of 10 tasks, and significantly outperforms static shielding approaches.

## Methodology
- **Neurosymbolic Policy Representation:** Defines two policy classes -- a symbolic class $\mathcal{G}$ of piecewise linear policies amenable to efficient verification, and a neurosymbolic class $\mathcal{H} \supseteq \mathcal{G}$ of blended policies of the form $(g, \phi, f)$. The safety monitor checks $P^\#(s, f(s)) \subseteq \phi$: if the neural action keeps the system within the inductive invariant $\phi$ under worst-case dynamics, the neural action $f(s)$ is executed; otherwise, the verified shield $g(s)$ is invoked.
- **Formal Verification via Abstract Interpretation:** Constructs inductive invariants $\phi$ as sets of hyperintervals (abstract states) using abstract interpretation, proving that the system cannot reach unsafe states $S_U$ under worst-case dynamics $P^\#$. Verification targets only the symbolic shield $g$, completely bypassing neural network verification.
- **Lift Step:** Promotes the current shield $g_t$ and its invariant $\phi_t$ into a neurosymbolic policy $(g_t, \phi_t, g_t)$, where the neural component is initialized by training a network to imitate $g_t$ via DAgger. This step is inherently safe, as safety depends solely on $g$ and $\phi$.
- **Update Step:** Applies policy gradient updates to the neurosymbolic policy $(g, \phi, f)$, modifying only the neural component $f$ while keeping $g$ and $\phi$ fixed, yielding $(g, \phi, f - \eta\nabla_\mathcal{F}J(h))$. Safety is preserved because the shield and monitor remain unchanged.
- **Project Step (Algorithm 2):** Iteratively refines the shield via a cutting plane method: at each iteration, a region is selected and split, and the IMITATESAFELY subroutine synthesizes a safe linear policy for each sub-region using constrained projected gradient descent to minimize imitation distance from the neurosymbolic policy while satisfying verification constraints. The procedure returns the shield with minimum Bregman divergence along with its corresponding new invariant.
- **IMITATESAFELY Subroutine:** Reduces safe imitation to a series of constrained supervised learning problems, alternating between gradient updates to a linear policy and projections onto a hyperinterval in parameter space. Abstract interpretation is used to verify that all controllers within each hyperinterval are safe, ensuring provable safety of the synthesized shield.
- **Experimental Setup:** 10 benchmarks (mountain-car, road, road-2d, noisy-road, noisy-road-2d, obstacle, obstacle2, pendulum, acc, car-racing) compared against DDPG, CPO, and static shielding baselines. REVEL achieves zero training safety violations across all benchmarks, matches unconstrained DDPG performance on most tasks, and spends approximately 87% of training time on shield synthesis.
