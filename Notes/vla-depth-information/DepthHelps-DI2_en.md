# Depth Helps: Improving Pre-trained RGB-based Policy with Depth Information Injection

## Topic
Depth injection for RGB policy

## Background
3D perception is crucial for generalizable robotic manipulation, yet most foundation-model-based manipulation policies rely solely on RGB input and therefore lack 3D awareness, limiting fine-grained manipulation. Training native RGB-D policies, in turn, depends on depth sensors and constrains deployment. Targeting the realistic situation where many capable RGB-based policies already exist and most deployment scenes are RGB-only, this paper injects the spatial prior carried by depth into a pre-trained RGB policy using minimal aligned RGB-D data, while keeping inference RGB-only. The base model is RoboFlamingo (a VLM architecture) pre-trained on Calvin; experiments use the LIBERO benchmark.

## Limitations & Research Problem
- **Limitation:** Mainstream robotic foundation policies use RGB only and lack 3D/depth perception, capping fine-grained manipulation. Native multimodal RGB-D policies and cross-modal distillation methods suffer from sequential accumulative errors in temporal manipulation (depth-prediction errors compound over time steps) and often over-emphasize cross-modality knowledge transfer while neglecting manipulation-specific requirements, which actually degrades RGB-only deployment performance.
- **Problem:** How can minimal aligned RGB-D data be used to inject depth's spatial prior into an already-trained RGB-based policy so that, at inference, RGB-only input yields 3D perception and control precision close to those of full RGB-D?

## Contributions
- Propose the Depth Information Injection (DI²) framework: fine-tune the policy with RGB-Depth data but rely solely on RGB images for deployment/inference, enhancing the 3D perception of a pre-trained RGB policy.
- Design two core modules — the Depth Completion Module (DCM) and the Depth-Aware Codebook (DAC) — respectively handling "predict depth features / inject spatial prior from RGB" and "discretize to denoise and curb cumulative error."
- Validate on the LIBERO simulation benchmark and in the real world (Franka Emika Panda + Intel D435, four tasks). Under RGB-only input the average success rate is 63.15%, about +5.2pp (≈9% relative) over the RGB-RF baseline (57.95%), with the largest margin on the Long Horizon suite (36.40% vs 24.20%).

## Methodology
- **Overall mechanism (train with RGB-D, infer with RGB only):** During training the model takes RGB-D and produces actions via `a_t = π(VLM(f_text, f_rgb, f_depth))`. At inference no real depth is available; the policy uses f_text, f_rgb, and a virtual depth feature predicted from RGB by the DCM and quantized by the DAC, feeding all three into the VLM. Depth thus enters the model as a *feature*, not an explicit depth map.
- **Depth Completion Module (DCM):** Enables the model to recover depth's spatial information without a depth input. RGB and depth are each passed through the same frozen ViT, then separate projection layers yield f_rgb and f_depth. Inspired by the Perceiver Resampler, k learnable tokens P ∈ R^{k×d} explicitly store the spatial prior: using f_rgb as the query, P concatenated with f_rgb forms the key/value, and several cross-attention + FFN layers fuse them to output the predicted depth feature f̂_depth = DCM(f_rgb, P). P encodes statistics shared between training and test scenes, letting the model infer manipulation-relevant depth from RGB.
- **Depth-Aware Codebook (DAC):** Addresses error accumulation in temporal manipulation, where predicted depth drifts from the ground-truth depth trajectory. A codebook Z ∈ R^{N×d} (N=512) quantizes the continuous depth feature from the DCM via nearest-neighbor lookup f̃_depth = q(f̂_depth | Z) = z_k (closest codeword by Euclidean distance). The codebook acts as clustering centroids of training-set depth features — more robust than the raw continuous features and able to filter modality-specific noise. To avoid codebook collapse it follows CVQ-VAE: each iteration updates codewords by a usage-frequency running average and re-initializes rarely-used codewords with anchor vectors (λ=0.99).
- **Three-stage training:** (1) Warm-up — jointly train an imitation policy on collected RGB+depth (action L2 loss; the depth branch is optimized together with the pre-trained RGB model). (2) Align — remove dependence on real depth: using the warm-up perceptual encoder and paired RGB-D data, train the DCM with `L_dcm = ||DCM(P, sg(f_rgb)) − sg(f_depth)||²` (sg = stop-gradient), distilling the 3D prior into the DCM. (3) Codebook — freeze the depth branch and train only the codebook with MSE loss, using running-average re-initialization to prevent collapse.
- **Quantitative gains:** In the RGB-D upper-bound experiment, Ours 63.95% vs RGB-RF 57.95% (+6pp). Switching to RGB-only, Ours drops only to 63.15% (smallest drop), whereas RGB-D-RF collapses from 61.25% to 15.65% (heavily depth-dependent). Ablations show the DCM contributes most (replacing it with an MLP yields only 36.95% average; full DCM+DAC reaches 63.15%); the DAC mainly improves Goal/Spatial/Long Horizon and reduces action-prediction error. Real-world four-task tests likewise confirm strong RGB-only performance.
