# SafeManip: A Property-Driven Benchmark for Temporal Safety Evaluation in Robotic Manipulation

> Authors: Chengyue Huang, Khang Vo Huynh, Sebastian Elbaum, Zsolt Kira, Lu Feng (2026, arXiv 2605.12386)

## Topic

Temporal safety benchmark for manipulation

## Background

Robotic manipulation has long been evaluated by task performance metrics such as success rate, but successful completion does not guarantee safe execution. Many safety failures are inherently *temporal*: a robot may touch a clean utensil after handling contaminated food, or release an object before it is fully inside an enclosure. These are not single unsafe states but failures that arise from how an execution unfolds over time. Existing safety benchmarks typically express safety through task-specific hazard labels, instantaneous collision checks, or cumulative trajectory costs, which obscure which safety rule was violated, when it was violated, and whether a task was completed safely or merely completed.

## Limitations & Research Problem

- **Limitation:** Safety semantics in existing benchmarks are fragmented and non-reusable. Hazard-oriented benchmarks (SafeBox, ResponsibleRobotBench, IS-Bench) specify electrical, fire, chemical, household, and human-safety risks via natural language or PDDL-like predicates but do not target low-level manipulation events such as grasping, contact, release, or containment. Execution-oriented benchmarks (SafeLIBERO, Safety-CHORES, FailureBench, RedVLA) express safety as instantaneous predicates, scalar costs, or red-teaming objectives rather than reusable temporal formulas over manipulation traces. Even the closest specification-driven efforts (VLA-Arena, SENTINEL) embed constraints inside task definitions and cost blocks, or state them mainly as general hazard rules over object categories and embodied-agent states. Prior specification-pattern libraries in robotics are largely mission-level or mobility-oriented, leaving no reusable property suite for manipulation safety.
- **Problem:** How can practical manipulation safety concerns be formalized as reusable, task-instantiable temporal properties that are monitorable over finite executions, so that task completion and safe execution can be measured separately and temporal safety failures of current VLA policies can be diagnosed at the property level?

## Contributions

- Introduces **SafeManip**, a policy-agnostic property-driven benchmark composed of three parts: reusable LTL_f safety property templates for manipulation, task-specific predicate bindings that map executions to symbolic traces, and metrics that separate task success from safe execution.
- Defines a suite of **10 LTL_f property templates across eight manipulation safety categories**: collision and contact safety, grasp stability, release stability, cross-contamination, action onset, mechanism recovery, object containment, and enclosure/access. Templates are written over abstract propositions and can be instantiated with task-specific objects, fixtures, regions, or skills, so the same specification generalizes across tasks and environments.
- Provides a **finite-trace monitoring protocol** that grounds rollout observations into symbolic predicate traces and evaluates LTL_f properties to yield property-level temporal safety outcomes, rather than binary safety labels or collision-style costs.
- Instantiates and evaluates the benchmark in RoboCasa on **50 RoboCasa365 tasks with six VLA policies and training variants** (π₀, π₀.₅, GR00T N1.5, plus GR00T-pt / GR00T-to / GR00T-tpt), running 50 rollouts per policy per task under a shared protocol, with no additional training or fine-tuning.
- Shows empirically that **task success masks temporal safety failures**: π₀.₅ improves success over π₀ from 8.1% to 9.3% while its violation rate rises from 69.7% to 82.8%; a large share of successful rollouts are success-but-unsafe; violations concentrate in collision/contact and release stability; and violation rates increase monotonically from atomic to long-horizon tasks and vary strongly by manipulation suite.
- Releases a public repository with property templates, task bindings, temporal monitors, rollout processing code, and evaluation scripts: https://github.com/chengyuehuang511/SafeManip

## Methodology

- **Specification language: LTL_f (Linear Temporal Logic over finite traces)**, chosen because manipulation rollouts are naturally finite (they terminate on success, failure, or horizon). Formulas are defined over atomic propositions denoting Boolean safety-relevant predicates (contact, grasp stability, fixture state, containment, contamination status), using ∧/∨/¬ together with Next (◯), Until (U), Always (□), and Eventually (◇).
- **Property template design.** Representative templates include φ₂: □(ObjGrasped → (StableGrasp U ObjReleased)) for grasp stability, φ₄: □(Contaminated → (¬CleanContact U Sanitized)) for hygiene ordering, and φ₆: □(MechHit → ◇(Retract ∧ ◇Recovered)) for mechanism recovery after fixture impact. The categories are informed by the hazard-oriented perspective of the OSHA Technical Manual and the hygiene/contamination perspective of the FDA Food Code, then adapted to finite robot executions. The suite deliberately spans per-timestep invariants as well as multi-step properties involving ordering, recovery, and eventual satisfaction.
- **Monitoring pipeline.** At each timestep the framework queries simulator state variables, object poses, contact events, gripper state, fixture state, and task-relevant action signals, and evaluates Boolean predicates (e.g. Contaminated, EnclosureCleared) that instantiate the abstract propositions. Each instantiated LTL_f formula is compiled into a **DFA** (via LTLf2DFA) and updated online as the symbolic trace is generated; a rollout is marked as violating when a monitor reaches a rejecting state, and the violation timestep, duration, and property category are recorded.
- **Task suite and policies.** SafeManip is instantiated in RoboCasa, which provides articulated fixtures and appliances while exposing simulator state for grounding symbolic predicates. The 50 RoboCasa365 tasks are grouped into seven manipulation suites (Atomic & Fixture; Beverage Preparation & Serving; Bread/Breakfast/Reheating; Cooking & Ingredient Preparation; Cleaning/Washing/Sanitation; Storage & Organization; Plating/Serving/Portioning). All six policy settings use externally provided checkpoints and share the same task suite, rollout protocol, temporal monitors, and metrics.
- **Evaluation metrics.** Task success rate; rollout-level and per-property safety violation rates; a decomposition of every rollout into **success-and-safe / success-but-unsafe / fail-but-safe / fail-and-unsafe**; and unsafe-state exposure rate, the percentage of timesteps spent in a violating state, which separates brief violations from prolonged unsafe behavior.
- **Exploratory prompt analysis.** Two safety-prompt variants were tested on GR00T-tpt. A short conservative-action prompt lowered success from 43.9% to 26.4% and violation rate from 71.8% to 69.4%; a longer prompt enumerating specific constraints pushed success down to 6.9% while reducing violations to 65.1%, indicating that prompt-based safety guidance can induce conservatism at substantial capability cost. Systematic prompt design is left to future work.

## Relevance to STL×VLA

SafeManip occupies roughly half of the "temporal logic for VLA safety" niche: it genuinely applies formal temporal specifications (LTL_f formulas compiled to DFAs and monitored online) to VLA policies, and it empirically establishes that task success and temporal safety are distinct axes — making it a strong motivating citation for any STL×VLA agenda. The space it leaves open is equally clear. **(1) Evaluation only, no enforcement.** Monitors report violations but never intervene in execution; there is no runtime shielding, no violation-triggered correction or replanning. The authors themselves list "incorporating property-level safety monitoring signals into safe policy training" as future work. **(2) LTL_f rather than STL.** Propositions are Boolean, so there is no real-valued robustness semantics and hence no differentiable degree-of-violation signal usable for constrained optimization or reward shaping — precisely the gap that STL's quantitative semantics fills. **(3) Predicate grounding relies on privileged simulator state** (object poses, contact events, fixture state); the paper explicitly notes that transferring the protocol to real robots would require reliable perception, contact estimation, fixture-state tracking, and task-event detection, leaving perception- or world-model-based estimation of predicates/robustness signals as an open interface. **(4) Properties are hand-authored templates with manual per-task bindings**, with no automatic synthesis of specifications from language instructions. In short, this work supplies the benchmark and measurement layer of the STL×VLA ecosystem — a solid failure taxonomy and evaluation infrastructure — while runtime STL enforcement, quantitative robustness, and specification-aware training remain unclaimed.
