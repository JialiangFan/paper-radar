# Underwater Object Detection - From Traditional to LVLM Survey

## Topic
UOD challenges, traditional to LVLMs

## Background
Underwater object detection (UOD) is critical for marine biodiversity monitoring, conservation, resource management and AUV/ROV-based autonomous operations, yet it suffers from light absorption, scattering, color cast, haze, motion blur and low contrast intrinsic to the submerged environment. These problems are compounded by scarce and imbalanced annotated datasets, frequent small/occluded targets and tight compute budgets on embedded marine platforms, making both traditional and modern detectors brittle. The recent rise of Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs), with proven competence in VQA, captioning and grounded localization, opens a new multimodal pathway to alleviate these limitations and is the central thread of this review.

## Limitations & Research Problem
- **Limitation:** Existing UOD reviews are dominated by classical and deep-learning methods and largely overlook how LVLMs could be leveraged for the underwater domain. State-of-the-art detectors still struggle with image degradation, small object detection, class imbalance, label noise, domain shift and real-time deployment on AUV embedded hardware. Public UOD datasets (RUOD, DUO, URPC, UDD, UODD, Brackish, WildFish, F4K-species, RF100) are limited in size, class coverage, annotation quality and environmental diversity.
- **Problem:** How can the multifaceted challenges of UOD be systematically categorized, and how can LVLMs - through synthetic data generation, efficient fine-tuning, multimodal grounding and sparse/efficient inference - be exploited to address data scarcity, domain shift, localization accuracy and real-time constraints in underwater scenes?

## Contributions
- A structured taxonomy that groups UOD challenges into five categories (Image Quality Degradation, Target-Related, Data-Related, Computational & Processing, Detection Methodology) and pairs each with both traditional and modern solutions (Fig. 1, Table 2).
- The first review to bring LVLMs (CLIP, Flamingo, BLIP-2, GPT-4V, Gemini, PaLM-E, MiniGPT-v2, InternVL, Qwen2-VL, LLaMA 3.2-vision, Florence-2, DeepSeekVL2, MoE-LLaVA, InternLM-XComposer2-4KHD, CoLLaVO, PIN, etc.) into the UOD discussion, mapping them to five UOD-relevant capabilities: image quality enhancement, target detection, synthetic data augmentation, real-time computation, and detection accuracy/localization.
- Two case studies validating LVLM utility for UOD: (i) synthetic data augmentation with DALL-E 3 plus OpenCV-based underwater effects (color transfer, Gaussian blur, blue/green haze) on top of Roboflow100, where YOLO11 trained on the combined 8800-image set improves mAP@50 (0.793 to 0.796), mAP@50-95 (0.501 to 0.505) and recall (0.714 to 0.736); (ii) LoRA-based efficient fine-tuning of Florence-2 LVLM for UOD.
- Three key insights: current UOD methods are insufficient for image degradation and small objects in dynamic scenes; LVLM-driven synthetic data is promising but needs more realism; LVLM real-time deployment for UOD remains under-explored.

## Methodology
- **Structured literature survey** anchored on a "challenges - current solutions - LVLM potential - case studies - future directions" taxonomy tree (Fig. 1), with examples illustrated on the DUO public dataset (imbalanced light, color deviation, small and occluded objects, low contrast, blur, hazing).
- **Challenge categorization** into: Image Quality Degradation (environmental effects, noise/distortion); Target-Related (small object detection, occlusions/overlapping objects, motion and dynamic scenes); Data-Related (dataset insufficiency, class imbalance, noisy labels, domain shift); Computational and Processing (real-time detection, preprocessing/enhancement, transfer learning and domain adaptation); Detection Methodology (bounding box issues, scale variation).
- **Survey of current solutions** along three axes: image processing (enhancement, restoration, fusion), image synthesis (GAN/diffusion-based), and object detection improvements (small-OD, bounding-box accuracy, scale handling, real-time, class imbalance, image-quality coupling, domain shift).
- **LVLM analysis (Section 4)**: cataloging recent LVLMs and their capabilities (Table 6), then matching them to UOD pain-points - using generative LVLMs (DALL-E, CoLLaVO) to ease data scarcity and class imbalance; using MoE-Tuning / MoE-LLaVA and InternLM-XComposer2-4KHD for efficient, high-resolution real-time inference suitable for AUVs; using PIN (Positional Insert) on frozen VLMs (Flamingo, GPT-4V) for spatial grounding without bounding-box supervision.
- **Case study 1 (Synthetic Data Augmentation pipeline)**: DALL-E 3 text-to-image and image-to-image generation for sea urchin / scallop / starfish / sea cucumber, refined via OpenCV (aesthetic, color transfer, blur, blue/green haze tints) into 1200 manually annotated synthetic images (Roboflow), combined with 7600 RF100 real images into an 8800-image hybrid dataset. YOLO11 is trained and compared on original vs combined sets, with PSNR/SSIM used to verify enhancement quality and a quality/diversity/complexity discussion of the synthetic data.
- **Case study 2 (Florence-2 fine-tuning)**: LoRA-based parameter-efficient fine-tuning of Florence-2 LVLM as a proof-of-concept that LVLMs can be adapted to UOD with limited compute.
- **Future directions** highlighted: automatic annotation for turbid scenes, prompt optimization for more realistic synthesis, lightweight LVLM deployment on AUV/ROV embedded hardware, and better cross-domain generalization for UOD.
