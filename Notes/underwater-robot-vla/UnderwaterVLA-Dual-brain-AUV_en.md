# UnderwaterVLA - Dual-brain VLA for Autonomous Underwater Navigation

## Topic
First VLA framework for AUVs

## Background
Autonomous Underwater Vehicles (AUVs) are central to seabed mapping, ecological monitoring, and subsea inspection, yet their autonomy is fundamentally limited by strong nonlinear hydrodynamics, turbulence and vortices, severe optical perception degradation in turbid water, and the bandwidth-limited, delay-prone nature of underwater acoustic communication. Conventional PID, adaptive model-based control, and reinforcement learning approaches generalize poorly in unstructured marine environments, while the emerging Vision-Language-Action (VLA) paradigm — successful in manipulation, autonomous driving, aerial robotics, and quadruped locomotion — has remained essentially unexplored for underwater robots.

## Limitations & Research Problem
- **Limitation:** Existing end-to-end VLA models lack an underwater-suited control hierarchy that can decouple high-level mission reasoning from low-level real-time control under stringent communication and compute budgets; they are also data-hungry, requiring large amounts of expensive underwater demonstrations, and they offer no real-time hydrodynamic compensation, since they embed neither physical priors nor online adaptation to fluid disturbances.
- **Problem:** How can a VLA framework be designed for AUVs such that it (i) remains robust under acoustic-bandwidth and onboard-compute constraints, (ii) eliminates the need for costly underwater demonstration data through a zero-data training paradigm, and (iii) explicitly compensates for nonlinear hydrodynamic effects at the actuation layer, so that language-conditioned navigation and obstacle-avoidance remain reliable under degraded underwater perception?

## Contributions
- Introduces UnderwaterVLA, claimed to be the first VLA framework specifically tailored to AUVs, systematically targeting underwater communication, perception, and data-collection bottlenecks.
- Proposes a biologically inspired dual-brain architecture: a cloud brain (QVQ-MAX-72B) performs intermittent long-horizon mission decomposition during surfacing events, while an on-device cerebellum (Qwen 2.5-VL-7B) executes step-wise closed-loop perception-action control, decoupling deliberative reasoning from reactive execution.
- Proposes a zero-data training methodology that leverages pre-trained multimodal foundation models with underwater-specific transfer learning, eliminating reliance on underwater demonstrations (262K samples for the QUAR-VLA baseline vs. 0 for UnderwaterVLA).
- Develops a hydrodynamics-informed Model Predictive Control (MPC) scheme that embeds added-mass and quadratic-drag effects into a phase-aware cost function and estimates drag coefficients online from IMU data, enabling real-time fluid compensation without task-specific training.
- Enforces structured Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning with a fixed JSON schema in both brains to support interpretability, logging, and failure diagnosis.
- Demonstrates +19% to +27% improvements over the QUAR-VLA baseline across perception, basic navigation, tunnel traversal, and obstacle-avoidance tasks, and shows graceful degradation versus a single-brain end-to-end model (SBM) under simulated low-light, high-turbidity conditions.

## Methodology
- **Dual-brain formalization:** the cloud brain mapping is written as $\Phi_{\text{cloud}}^{\text{QVQmax}} \to \{S_1,\dots,S_N\}$, producing a sequence of high-level sub-goals, while the cerebellum mapping $\Phi_{\text{local}}^{\text{Qwen-VL}} \to \mathcal{J}_t$ outputs a JSON-formatted control decision at each timestep $t$. The cloud brain runs only intermittently at surfacing events; the cerebellum runs continuously on-device.
- **Prompt engineering and CoT schema:** both brains are prompted to emit explicit reasoning traces. The cerebellum outputs a strict JSON tuple with mandatory fields `reasoning`, `decision`, `velocity`, `sub_task_done`, `mission_done`, enabling consistent logging and runtime monitoring.
- **Discrete action space:** direction $\mathcal{D}\in\{$forward, backward, left, right$\}$ and velocity level $\mathcal{V}\in\{$low, medium, high$\}$, mapped to translational targets of 0.2/0.5/0.8 m/s and rotational targets of 0.5/1.0/1.5 rad/s.
- **Time-constrained motion profile:** every discrete action is executed in exactly 1 s with a three-phase reference trajectory — acceleration (0–0.2 s), constant velocity (0.2–0.5 s), and deceleration (0.5–1.0 s) — with separate $v(t)$ and $r(t)$ profiles for translation and rotation.
- **Hydrodynamics-informed MPC:** thrust $\tau$ is optimized to minimize a mode-dependent cost $\beta\|v-v_{\text{ref}}\|^2 + \gamma\|\tau\|^2 + \delta\|F_{\text{drag},v}\|^2$ (and the analogous rotational form), with quadratic drag modeled as $F_{\text{drag},v}=D_v v|v|$ and $\tau_{\text{drag},r}=D_r r|r|$.
- **Online drag estimation:** $\hat D_v=(\tau_v - M\dot v)/(v|v|)$ and $\hat D_r=(\tau_r - I_z \dot r)/(r|r|)$ are computed from IMU acceleration and angular acceleration measurements, providing real-time fluid adaptation without offline training.
- **MPC execution loop:** the controller selects translation or turning mode from the VLA command, runs at $\delta t = 0.02$ s control intervals, allocates thrusts via the pseudo-inverse $\mathbf u_t = \mathbf B^{\dagger}\tau_t^{*}$, and finishes with active braking until $\|v\|<0.01$ m/s or $|r|<0.01$ rad/s.
- **Phase-specific fluid-structure handling:** the acceleration phase compensates for added mass $M_A=\rho\nabla C_A$, the constant-velocity phase handles quadratic drag with $F_D=\tfrac{1}{2}\rho C_D A v^2$ (and the rotational analogue), and the deceleration phase exploits natural fluid damping for smooth braking.
- **Experimental validation:** a vision-guided navigation case study in a tank with three cylindrical obstacles demonstrates sequential sub-goal generation, lateral detour, and goal closure. Robustness is evaluated by progressively dimming illumination and injecting 5–200 µm diatomaceous earth (turbidity from 0.5 to 18 NTU; ~65% contrast reduction at 1.5 m), comparing the dual-brain model (DBM) against a single-brain end-to-end model (SBM). Quantitative results compare against QUAR-VLA's reported simulation numbers across four tasks, with UnderwaterVLA using zero training samples.
- **Caveats observed when skimming:** experiments are conducted in a controlled tank rather than open ocean; the quantitative baseline is a simulation result rather than a like-for-like real-world reimplementation; sample sizes are small (e.g., 4/5 and 3/5); and the operational integration of the cloud brain over a real underwater acoustic link is not fully justified.
