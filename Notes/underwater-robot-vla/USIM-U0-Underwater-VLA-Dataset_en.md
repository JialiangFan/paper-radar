# USIM and U0 - A VLA Dataset and Model for Underwater Robots

## Topic
Underwater VLA dataset and model

## Background
Underwater environments present unique challenges for robotic operation, including complex hydrodynamics, limited visibility, and constrained communication. Although data-driven approaches have advanced embodied intelligence on terrestrial robots and task-specific autonomous underwater vehicles, large-scale, high-quality underwater datasets that enable general multi-task underwater intelligence remain scarce. This paper builds on the Stonefish simulator and the BlueROV2 platform to introduce USIM, the first multi-task Vision-Language-Action (VLA) dataset for underwater robots, and U0, a general underwater VLA model trained on it.

## Limitations & Research Problem
- **Limitation:** Existing underwater datasets such as VAROS, UIEB, AQUALOC, and the Underwater Caves Sonar Data Set are largely task-specific and aimed at perception, providing no unified VLA data. Real-world underwater data collection is costly and risky. While simulators (HoloOcean, Dave, FishGym, Stonefish) exist, they have not been used to construct large-scale multi-task VLA datasets. Indoor/terrestrial VLA models (RT-2, OpenVLA, π0, GR00T N1.5) suffer a large domain gap when applied directly to underwater scenes, and monocular vision is insufficient for accurate underwater 3D spatial perception.
- **Problem:** How can we build a scalable, high-quality underwater VLA dataset and train a general underwater VLA model that jointly handles visual navigation, obstacle avoidance, inspection, scanning, dynamic tracking, and fluid-dynamics-aware mobile manipulation under language instructions?

## Contributions
- Constructs the USIM dataset on the Stonefish simulator with a BlueROV2 platform: 561K frames, 1,852 trajectories, ~15.6 hours of robot-environment interaction, covering 9 scenarios (seabed, subsea pipeline, industrial pool, solar charging station, lake, open sea surface, underwater factory, modern shipwreck, ancient shipwreck) and 20 tasks (12 grasping, 2 pipeline inspection, 2 shipwreck scanning, 2 obstacle-avoidance navigation, 1 dynamic tracking, 1 transport), formatted to the LeRobot specification.
- Proposes U0, a general underwater VLA model built on the Isaac-GR00T N1.5 backbone (~3B parameters) that fuses binocular vision with IMU, pressure, DVL, altitude, joint, and thruster signals, and adds a VLM-feature-guided Convolution-Attention-based Perception focus enhancement (CAP) module.
- Establishes a scalable data-to-task framework: the first systematic effort to jointly address multi-task underwater perception and action under language guidance, achieving an 80% average success rate on inspection, obstacle avoidance, scanning, and dynamic tracking, and reducing the robot-to-target distance by 21.2% over the baseline on challenging mobile grasping tasks.
- The 3B-parameter model is lightweight enough for embedded AI platforms such as NVIDIA Jetson, supporting practical deployment of underwater embodied intelligence.

## Methodology
- **Simulation environment construction:** Nine underwater scenes are built in the Stonefish simulator with a BlueROV2 equipped with a manipulator and parallel gripper, supporting hydrodynamics, collisions, and grasping. ROS integration is provided via the Stonefish ROS package, with map randomization, randomized sunlight, and varied Jerlov water clarity to enrich visual diversity.
- **Data collection pipeline:** An automated parallel collection system records data at 10 Hz; a PID controller handles ROV pose tracking, while MoveIt plans and controls the manipulator for grasping. The dataset is split into 526K frames / 1,752 trajectories for training and 35K frames / 100 trajectories for testing.
- **U0 model architecture:** A dual-system design. A vision encoder plus projector ingests stereo images and a text tokenizer processes language instructions, both feeding the VLM. ROV state (IMU, pressure, DVL, altitude, joint, thruster) is encoded and combined with VLM action tokens inside a Diffusion Transformer that generates actions via cross-attention. In parallel, VLM features feed the CAP module that predicts the target as an auxiliary branch, which can be disabled at inference to avoid added latency.
- **Multi-source sensor fusion and action space:** The state space combines binocular images with IMU, pressure, DVL, altitude, joints, and thrusters. The action space concatenates normalized thruster PWM signals with manipulator joint angles. Target poses are expressed in a robot-centric frame as p_{t2r} = (R_r^T R_t, R_r^T (t_t - t_r)), reducing dependence on a fixed world frame and aligning with egocentric reasoning.
- **CAP module:** Tokens from the binocular VLM pass through 1-D Conv, BatchNorm, ReLU, and a MASK-aware convolution, then a channel-wise attention path (1-D Conv + BatchNorm + sigmoid), followed by pooling and an MLP that outputs the predicted target. Training uses an MSE loss L_CAP, combined with the action loss as L = L_action + α L_CAP.
- **Training setup:** Total batch size 1024 for 5000 steps; GR00T N1.5 and U0 are fine-tuned separately on monocular and binocular variants of USIM.
- **Evaluation:** Open-loop offline evaluation on the 35K-frame test set compares GR00T N1.5, GR00T FT, and U0 on action error e_action and target error e_target under monocular and binocular inputs. Closed-loop online testing executes 5 grasping tasks and 7 non-grasping tasks in the simulator, reporting success rates and mean robot-to-target distance.
