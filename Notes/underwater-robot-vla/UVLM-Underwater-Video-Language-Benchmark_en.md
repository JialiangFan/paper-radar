# UVLM - Video Language Model Benchmark for Underwater Understanding

## Topic
Underwater video-language benchmark

## Background
Video-language models (VidLMs) have made rapid progress on terrestrial video captioning, temporal grounding, and visual question answering, but their training and evaluation corpora are overwhelmingly centered on human-centric activities and common objects. Underwater applications such as marine biodiversity monitoring, ecosystem health assessment, autonomous underwater vehicle (AUV) operation, and offshore infrastructure inspection demand high-level semantic understanding of underwater video, yet no benchmark exists to measure or improve VidLMs in this special imaging condition. UVLM (Xue et al., AAAI 2026, arXiv:2507.02373), from Northwestern Polytechnical University and Intellifusion, is proposed to fill this gap.

## Existing Limitations and Research Questions
- **Limitation:** Existing underwater datasets (LSUI, DRUVA, UIIS, VMAT, WebUOT, MarineInst, USOD, etc.) target only low-level vision tasks such as image enhancement, detection, segmentation, or single-object tracking, with no video-level language annotations. Mainstream VidLM training corpora contain almost no underwater content, so off-the-shelf VidLMs lack marine biology domain knowledge and struggle with degraded visual features (light attenuation, turbidity, color shift, occlusion), causing significant performance drops when transferred to subaquatic scenes.
- **Problem:** Can current VidLMs effectively understand videos captured under special imaging conditions, particularly underwater environments? How can we build a video-language benchmark that faithfully captures typical underwater challenges and rich fine-grained ecological knowledge, so as to systematically evaluate and substantially improve VidLMs for underwater world understanding?

## Contributions
- Introduces UVLM, the first underwater video-language multimodal benchmark, covering oceans, lakes, and rivers along with typical degradation conditions (light attenuation, turbidity, scattering, occlusion, etc.).
- Scale: ~2,109 videos, 0.86M frames, 419 fine-grained marine organism categories organized along the classical five-kingdom taxonomy (kingdom/phylum/class/order/...). Each video carries 16–20 video-language pairs, totaling roughly 40k video-text pairs.
- Task design: observation targets are divided into biological and environmental classes, each split into content observation and change/action observation, yielding 20 subtasks that map to four core technical challenges—temporal understanding, fine-grained recognition, compositional reasoning, and knowledge-grounded generation.
- Evaluation suite: 8 metrics, including 2 objective metrics (Multiple Choice Accuracy MCA, Fine-grained Taxonomic Classification FGC) and 5 LLM-judge metrics (Semantic Accuracy SA, Detail Completeness DC, Visual Perception Accuracy VPA, Environmental Description Accuracy EDA, Species Behavior Matching SBM), aggregated into an overall accuracy.
- Empirical findings: fine-tuning on UVLM yields large gains for multiple VidLMs (e.g., VideoLLaMA3-7B improves overall accuracy by +10.34 to 73.04, closing the gap to Qwen2.5VL-72B and Gemini); the benefit transfers to general benchmarks (VideoMME, Perception Test); and on the FGC task, scaling data alone cannot bridge the gap between small and large models, exposing a remaining open challenge in specialized scientific reasoning.

## Methodology
- Dual-path video collection: (a) crawl real underwater videos from YouTube and Bilibili under two strict rules—both camera and observed targets must be underwater, and the dataset must cover typical underwater challenges and target diversity—producing ~400 sequences with 53 target classes; (b) re-annotate the existing WebUOT tracking dataset for scalability. Clip lengths range from 100 to 3,000 frames, including long videos to probe long-horizon temporal understanding.
- Data cleaning: SAM is used to segment subtitles, watermarks, and other interfering regions, and LaMa inpaints them; aquarium, fish-tank, and game-rendered footage are filtered out; very long videos are split into 300–600 frame clips to balance the distribution.
- Manual annotation: 12 annotators use the DarkLabel tool to draw axis-aligned bounding boxes on each frame, following explicit rules for occlusion handling, sharply protruding parts, and visibility ratios; every sequence is verified by three domain experts.
- Three-phase fine-grained taxonomic labeling: 4 annotators with marine biology backgrounds independently label species and the full five-kingdom taxonomy (cross-checked with Wikipedia and other authoritative sources); annotator pairs cross-validate and disagreements go to a third annotator; a senior marine biology expert re-reviews flagged cases; finally, a panel of 5 experts collectively resolves remaining ambiguities to obtain definitive labels.
- Model-assisted QA generation: human annotations are injected as priors into prompts that instruct GPT-4o to produce 16–20 multiple-choice and open-ended questions per video along two dimensions—Marine Organism Dimension (static: species identification, morphology, coloration; dynamic: behavior, feeding, interactions, movement) and Underwater Environment Dimension (static: substrates, coral structures, habitat; dynamic: lighting variation, visibility fluctuations). Topic and style constraints enforce taxonomic correctness and linguistic diversity.
- Two-tier human correction: a first round of general reviewers checks consistency between the prompt-supplied information and GPT-4o output; a second round of senior experts performs in-depth edits for factual precision and domain conformity, removing or rewriting hallucinated or context-irrelevant content.
- Experimental protocol: training-test split is class-stratified, with a 208-pair test set; UVLM fine-tuning is applied to InternVL2.5 (1B/8B), Qwen2.5VL (2B/7B/72B), VideoLLaMA3 (2B/7B), and Qvis2.5-2B, then compared against closed-source models GPT-4o, Claude3.7-Sonnet, and Gemini2.5-Flash on both UVLM and the general VideoMME and Perception Test benchmarks, demonstrating that UVLM fine-tuning improves underwater understanding while also lifting general video understanding performance.
