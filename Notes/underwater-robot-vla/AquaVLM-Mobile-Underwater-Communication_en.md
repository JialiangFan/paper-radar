# AquaVLM - Mobile VLM for Underwater Situation Awareness

## Topic
Mobile VLM underwater messaging

## Background
Tens of millions of people engage in scuba diving and other underwater activities every year, and maintaining situational awareness through reliable diver-to-diver communication is critical for safety. Traditional channels such as hand signals or flash signals are device-free but information-poor; underwater talking devices and messaging-capable diving computers are bulky and cost over \$1000; recent smartphone-based acoustic apps (e.g., AquaApp) are ubiquitous and cheap but can only deliver short, predefined text messages that cannot capture context-specific observations. The paper, by Beitong Tian, Lingzhi Zhao et al. from University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign, will appear at ACM Multimedia Systems 2026 in Hong Kong (arXiv:2510.21722).

## Limitations & Research Problem
- **Limitation:** No existing personal underwater communication method is simultaneously *ubiquitous* and *informative*. Hand/flash signals work below 10 m and only convey predefined gestures; underwater talking devices are expensive and intrusive; diving-computer messaging is costly and capped at ~10 preset messages; smartphone apps such as AquaApp are accessible but restricted to predefined text. Even if a mobile VLM is deployed on the phone, two further obstacles remain: (1) commodity mobile VLMs lack underwater domain knowledge and cannot generate diving-relevant conversations without fine-tuning, and large-scale underwater multimodal corpora do not exist; (2) underwater acoustic links suffer from high bit error rates that corrupt transmitted text, and current VLMs implicitly assume clean inputs, so reliability degrades sharply.
- **Problem:** *Can divers share context-rich underwater information effortlessly using only their mobile devices to improve situational awareness?* Concretely, how can we run a mobile VLM on a smartphone enclosed in a waterproof pouch—where typing is infeasible—to automatically generate context-aware, purpose-aligned messages from images and sensor data, transmit them reliably over a noisy acoustic channel, and recover them on the receiver side, all within tight latency, screen, and compute budgets?

## Contributions
- Introduces **AquaVLM**, the first underwater communication system that leverages a mobile VLM to enable a "tap-and-send" interaction paradigm: the diver merely selects a purpose and the phone generates and transmits image- and sensor-grounded messages over acoustic signals.
- Designs a **context-aware instruction-tuning pipeline** that synthesizes a multimodal underwater conversation corpus with ChatGPT-4o, organized into three sub-tasks (sender message generation, reply generation, message recovery) and four communication purposes (safety, navigation, environment, equipment), totaling ~85k LoRA fine-tuning samples (16k + 21k + 48k). A *hierarchical message generation* scheme conditioned on the chosen purpose reduces the candidate set from 8 to 2, cutting computation while preserving relevance.
- Proposes **error-resilient fine-tuning**, where character-level corruptions matching real PHY-layer bit error rates (0–20% BER under CSS chirp modulation) are injected into training pairs so that the VLM learns to recover meaning from corrupted text—the first work to harden a VLM specifically for noisy text transmission.
- Builds and evaluates two complementary platforms: a **Unity-based VR simulator on Meta Quest 3** with four immersive scenarios (Shark Encounter, Tank Leakage, Shipwreck Discovery, Kelp Forest Exploration) and a **fully functional iOS prototype** (iPhone 12 Pro + Apple Watch Ultra, 4-bit MobileVLMV2-3B via llama.cpp/LLMFarm). User studies (n=20) and real-world lake experiments demonstrate 70–80% purpose-align rate, BER below 3% within 20 m, and >90% semantic similarity within 15 m, outperforming an AquaApp-based baseline.

## Methodology
- **System overview.** AquaVLM has an offline stage—instruction-tuning a commodity mobile VLM with auto-generated underwater conversations—and an online stage in which the tuned VLM runs on each diver's phone, ingests the camera image and watch sensor data, generates two candidate messages tied to the selected purpose, encodes them for acoustic transmission, and on the receiver side decodes, recovers, and prompts a reply.
- **Multimodal data preparation.** 1,368 keyframes are extracted from five YouTube scuba videos covering diverse sites; nine critical sensor parameters (depth, temperature, tank pressure, NDL, etc.) are synthesized via a sensor simulator that mimics descent, exploration, ascent, safety stop, and completion phases.
- **Conversation generation with ChatGPT-4o.** Prompts (with concise rules and few-shot examples) ask GPT-4o to (1) generate two sender messages from diver #1's intent, image, and sensor data, then (2) produce two replies from diver #2 conditioned on diver #2's intent and sensors. Splitting into focused subtasks acts like chain-of-thought, increases diversity, and cuts formatting errors.
- **Instruction tuning.** Three tasks—sender generation (16k), reply generation (21k), and message recovery (48k)—are unified into ~85k instruction–answer samples. The mobile VLM (MobileVLMV2-3B) is fine-tuned with **LoRA** (rank 64, lr 4e-5, cosine schedule with warm-up ratio 0.03, AdamW) on the language component only, with the vision encoder frozen, on 2× NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPUs for 1 epoch (~4 hours).
- **Hierarchical message generation.** Four purpose categories—safety, navigation, environment, equipment—are injected into the prompt to narrow the search to two candidates (instead of eight), reducing latency. A threshold-based filter keeps only abnormal or potentially hazardous sensor readings (e.g., water temperature <15 °C or tank pressure <700 psi), implicitly encoding general scuba knowledge into the input.
- **Error-resilient fine-tuning.** A PHY-layer pipeline (encode → modulate → demodulate → decode) is built around CSS chirp spread spectrum (SF=5, BW=2 kHz, 312 bps); numbers are spelled out ("12.1" → "twelve point one") to increase robustness. BER is swept from 0% to 20% in 1% steps; each level is applied to a random subset of generated sender/reply pairs, yielding 48,000 corrupted–original message pairs that train the VLM to repair character-level corruption.
- **VR simulation platform.** Built in Unity for the Meta Quest 3 with photorealistic 3D assets and real underwater audio. Four event scripts trigger randomly. To avoid headset stutter, frames are rendered on a backend server and streamed over AirLink; "swimming"-style hand gestures replace controllers; latency from real iPhone traces is emulated for realism; interaction logs are saved for future customization.
- **iOS prototype.** Implemented in Swift/SwiftUI with the Metal API and Accelerate; the Apple Watch Ultra streams sensor data to the iPhone 12 Pro acoustically. The phone sits in a touch-sensitive waterproof pouch. MobileVLMV2-3B is quantized to 4 bits, converted to GGUF via llama.cpp, and deployed through LLMFarm.
- **Transmission pipeline.** 4/7 Hamming channel coding, CSS modulation (SF=5, BW=2 kHz, 312 bps), training symbols every three data symbols for time equalization, and a preamble for packet detection; the receiver continuously listens, synchronizes, equalizes, demodulates, decodes, then hands the recovered text to the VLM-based recovery module.
- **Evaluation.**
  - *User study (n=20, 13M/7F, ages 18–35).* After a 5-min tutorial, participants explored VR with 2–3 random events and conversed with a simulated buddy via AquaVLM, then completed a 9-item, 1–5 MOS questionnaire. Purpose-align rate ranges 70–80%, highest for the casual category and lowest for status. MOS ratings for interaction, immersiveness, and overall experience were satisfactory; responsiveness scored lower because messaging latency contrasts with users' walkie-talkie expectations.
  - *Real-world tests* in a lake (max range 20 m, average depth 3 m, sender/receiver mics at 2 m). Compared against an AquaApp+ baseline using a three-step OFDM protocol, AquaVLM keeps BER below 3% within 20 m and semantic similarity above 90% within 15 m. A second 200-pair user study sets 92% as the semantic-equivalence threshold. Performance is robust across depth, orientation, and motion (BER < 2% even under 2–2.5 m/s² accelerations).
  - *Metrics.* Purpose-align rate, responsiveness, interaction, immersiveness, overall MOS, BER, semantic cosine similarity (all-MiniLM-L6-v2), and one-round end-to-end latency, where VLM inference and acoustic transmission dominate the latency budget.
